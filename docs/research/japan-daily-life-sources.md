# Japan daily life and local laws: official sources (for Packy)

Read 26 Nov 2026 from Japan's official tourism site (JNTO, https://www.japan.travel/en/), which the cloud session can
reach. Quotes are exact. Gap: the legal drinking age is not stated on these pages; Packy does not give one until an
official page is added here.

## Buying alcohol
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/gastronomy/sake/ (JNTO, "Japanese Sake & Breweries", checked 26 Nov 2026)
- "If you fancy a drink in the comfort of your hotel or the great outdoors, you can also buy drinks from sakaya (sake
  shops), supermarkets and, even convenience stores."
- "Izakaya, or Japanese pubs, serve a variety of food that you can enjoy with your drinks."
- Sake "is Japan's national drink"; "Sake is traditionally drunk from small cups called ochoko, poured from decanters
  called tokkuri."
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/japan-magazine/2002_konbini/ (JNTO magazine, convenience stores, checked 26 Nov 2026)
- Convenience stores are called konbini. "Chu-his are alcoholic drinks made from shochu (a Japanese spirit) and flavored
  mixer. They come in a variety of alcohol strengths ranging from 3% alcohol to 12%."

## Drinking in public
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/local-laws/ (JNTO, "Local Laws", checked 26 Nov 2026)
- "Drinking outside on the other hand doesn't violate any laws—in fact, it's part of the culture, especially during the
  hanami season in spring when picnics under the cherry blossoms are common."
- "However, while this might be the case, loud and boisterous behavior is not part of Japanese culture, so try to keep
  any outdoor partying under control. Be sure to take any litter home with you."

## Carry your passport
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/local-laws/ (checked 26 Nov 2026)
- "Be sure to carry your passport with you at all times as a form of identification. The chances are you won't need to
  show it, but there is always a chance that you may be required to do so."

## Smoking
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/local-laws/ (checked 26 Nov 2026)
- "Smoking is not allowed in most restaurants and bars, except in designated areas. This rule is strictly
  enforced—especially in the country's main cities—and can result in a fine, so don't walk and smoke, find a smoking
  area."

## Tattoos (onsen, public baths, pools, gyms)
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/local-laws/ (checked 26 Nov 2026)
- "Many public swimming pools, public baths, hot spring resorts, and gyms prohibit entry to anyone with tattoos—even
  small ones. The rules are loosening slightly, and some establishments will simply ask you to cover any tattoos up.
  Research in advance to avoid any disappointment."

## Drugs and cannabis
Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/local-laws/ (checked 26 Nov 2026)
- "There is zero tolerance towards any kind of drug-related crime." "Cannabis is treated in the same way as other
  drugs. Possession of a small amount can lead to a prison sentence, so please don't bring cannabis into the country;
  even if it is prescribed in your country of origin."
- National Police Agency warning (PDF): https://www.npa.go.jp/english/bureau/criminal_affairs/image/warning_illegal_drugs_in_japan.pdf
