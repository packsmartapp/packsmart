# Japan basics: official-source facts for the "Ask about Japan" assistant

Research notes, 24 Nov 2026. Every bullet is a short quote or close paraphrase of an official page (JNTO japan.travel, JR West, airport operators, Kyoto City and Osaka tourism bureaus), with its URL. Facts were fetched in a cloud sandbox. Government hosts (mofa.go.jp, jma.go.jp, mlit.go.jp, customs.go.jp, digital.go.jp), JR Central, JR East, JR Kyushu, japanrailpass.net, Centrair and Naha Airport were **blocked** (see the end), so those facts come from JNTO pages that link to them, and the official URL is given for the reader to check.

Rules for the assistant: travel times are approximate and depend on the train. Where two official pages differ, both are given, marked **Differs**. No prices are repeated here except where a page states a rule tied to one (noted).

## 1. Regions and main cities

- JNTO splits Japan into ten travel regions: Hokkaido, Tohoku, Hokuriku Shinetsu, Kanto, Tokai, Kansai, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu, Okinawa. JNTO does not use "Chubu" or "Kinki": Chubu is split into Hokuriku Shinetsu and Tokai, and Kinki is called Kansai. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/ (site menu), checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Prefectures by region (JNTO menu): **Hokkaido**; **Tohoku**: Aomori, Akita, Iwate, Yamagata, Miyagi, Fukushima; **Hokuriku Shinetsu**: Niigata, Toyama, Ishikawa, Fukui, Nagano; **Kanto**: Tokyo, Kanagawa, Chiba, Saitama, Ibaraki, Tochigi, Gunma; **Tokai**: Yamanashi, Shizuoka, Gifu, Aichi, Mie; **Kansai**: Kyoto, Osaka, Shiga, Hyogo, Nara, Wakayama; **Chugoku**: Tottori, Shimane, Okayama, Hiroshima, Yamaguchi; **Shikoku**: Tokushima, Kagawa, Ehime, Kochi; **Kyushu**: Fukuoka, Saga, Nagasaki, Oita, Kumamoto, Miyazaki, Kagoshima; **Okinawa**. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/ (site menu), checked 24 Nov 2026.
- "Major cities" per region (JNTO menu): Hokkaido: Sapporo, Asahikawa, Hakodate, Kushiro. Tohoku: Sendai, Aomori, Akita, Morioka, Tsuruoka, Fukushima. Hokuriku Shinetsu: Kanazawa, Niigata, Toyama, Nagano, Matsumoto, Fukui. Kanto: Tokyo, Yokohama, Saitama, Chiba, Utsunomiya, Maebashi. Tokai: Nagoya, Fujiyoshida, Hamamatsu, Gifu, Takayama, Iseshima. Kansai: Osaka, Kyoto, Nara, Kobe, Himeji, Otsu. Chugoku: Hiroshima, Okayama, Kurashiki, Matsue, Tottori, Shimonoseki. Shikoku: Matsuyama, Takamatsu, Tokushima, Kochi. Kyushu: Fukuoka, Kumamoto, Kagoshima, Beppu, Nagasaki, Miyazaki. Okinawa: Naha, Ishigaki Island, Miyako Island, Kerama Islands. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/ (site menu), checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Geography: Japan "stretches from the northernmost island of Hokkaido, close to Russia, to the subtropics of Okinawa"; it is "a curved-shaped archipelago with the Sea of Japan along the west coast and the Pacific Ocean along the east coast"; mountains and forest cover "70 percent of the country". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/weather/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- The JR network connects "Hokkaido, Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu, the four main islands of Japan". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/jr-rail-pass/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Hokkaido**: "Japan's northernmost prefecture"; "Sapporo, Hokkaido's main city"; Hakodate "a traditional port". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/hokkaido/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tohoku**: "Japan's northeastern wilderness is a huge region encompassing six rural prefectures"; "Much more rural than Tokyo yet more developed than Hokkaido". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tohoku/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Kanto**: "Anchored by Tokyo, the world's biggest metropolis"; Yokohama and Gunma, Saitama and Ibaraki "are right on Tokyo's doorstep". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kanto/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Hokuriku Shinetsu**: "the convenient high-speed bullet train connecting Tokyo to most of Hokuriku Shinetsu in two and a half hours or less"; Ishikawa's capital is Kanazawa; "Nagano is the only one without a coastline". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/hokuriku-shinetsu/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokai**: "Served by the shinkansen between Tokyo and Kyoto"; includes Mt. Fuji, Hakone, Shirakawa-go and Ise-Jingu. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tokai/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Kansai**: "Japan's spiritual and cultural capital"; "The nation's ancient capitals of Kyoto and Nara"; Osaka nightlife and the Kobe cityscape. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kansai/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Osaka is "an excellent base for exploring the greater Kansai region, which includes Kyoto, Nara, Kobe and Wakayama. Each of these areas is a one-hour train ride from Osaka's central transportation hubs". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kansai/osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Chugoku**: Hiroshima "is merely the entry point to western Japan"; "Continue west following the Sanyo Shinkansen". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/chugoku/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Shikoku**: "the smallest of Japan's four major islands". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/shikoku/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Kyushu**: "Japan's third-largest island"; Fukuoka is its startup hub. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Fukuoka is "Japan's fifth largest city"; Hakata and Tenjin are its two halves. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/fukuoka/fukuoka-city/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Okinawa**: "Japan's southernmost prefecture, Okinawa is a chain of islands" with "a distinctly subtropical climate". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/okinawa/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Kyoto "is located almost in the center of Japan". Source: https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Nagoya (Aichi, Tokai) is "Located in the middle of Honshu ... a key transportation hub". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tokai/aichi/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

## 2. Distances and travel times between major cities

Shinkansen lines (JNTO): Tokaido: Tokyo, Shin-Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto, Shin-Osaka. Sanyo: Shin-Osaka, Hiroshima, Hakata. Kyushu: Hakata to Kagoshima-Chuo. Nishi Kyushu: Takeo-Onsen to Nagasaki. Tohoku: Tokyo to Shin-Aomori via Sendai and Morioka. Hokkaido: Shin-Aomori to Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto. Hokuriku: Tokyo to Nagano, Kanazawa and Tsuruga. Joetsu: Tokyo to Niigata. Akita: Tokyo to Akita. Yamagata: Tokyo to Shinjo. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/shinkansen/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

- **Tokyo to Kyoto** (Tokaido Shinkansen): "approximately 130 minutes ... on the Nozomi bullet train, or 170 minutes on the Hikari bullet train", no transfer. Source: https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/, checked 24 Nov 2026. **Differs:** JNTO FAQ says "roughly 2 hours from Tokyo to Kyoto". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Osaka (Shin-Osaka)** (Tokaido Shinkansen): "approximately two and a half hours", then JR local line to Osaka Station. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kansai/osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Osaka's tourism bureau also gives Tokyo to Shin-Osaka "Shinkansen (2 hours 30 minutes)" and says JR and Osaka Metro run between Shin-Osaka and Osaka stations "every 5 to 10 minutes". Source: https://osaka-info.jp/en/information/to-osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026. **Differs:** JNTO FAQ says "3 hours to Osaka". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Tokyo to Osaka by plane: Haneda to Osaka (Itami) "1 hour 20 minutes"; Haneda to Kansai International "1 hour 10 minutes". Source: https://osaka-info.jp/en/information/to-osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026. By highway bus: Tokyo to Osaka "approximately 8 hours 30 minutes to 9 hours"; by car "approx. 6 hours". Same source.
- **Tokyo to Nagoya**: bullet trains "an hour and 40 minutes from Tokyo"; Nagoya is 35 minutes from Kyoto and 50 minutes from Osaka. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tokai/aichi/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Nagoya to Kyoto "approximately 34 minutes ... on the Nozomi ... or 43 minutes on the Hikari". Source: https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Kobe (Shin-Kobe)**: "between 160 and 195 minutes depending on the train" (Tokaido/Sanyo Shinkansen). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kansai/hyogo/kobe-and-around/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Hiroshima**: "by shinkansen in around four hours"; "Haneda Airport to Hiroshima Airport takes around 90 minutes". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/chugoku/hiroshima/hiroshima-city/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Line: Tokaido then Sanyo Shinkansen. A Nozomi lets you "ride from Tokyo to Hiroshima without changing trains" (with a JR Pass this needs an extra ticket). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/jr-rail-pass/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Osaka to Hiroshima** (Sanyo Shinkansen): "about 90 minutes from Osaka". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/chugoku/hiroshima/hiroshima-city/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Hiroshima to Shin-Osaka "Shinkansen (1 hour 30 minutes)"; highway bus "approximately 5-6 hours". Source: https://osaka-info.jp/en/information/to-osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Hiroshima to Fukuoka (Hakata)**: "It takes one hour by shinkansen from Hiroshima to Fukuoka City". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/fukuoka/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Kanazawa** (Hokuriku Shinkansen): "about 2 hours 30 minutes on the Hokuriku Shinkansen from Tokyo Station, which stops at Nagano and Toyama stations". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/hokuriku-shinetsu/ishikawa/kanazawa-and-around/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Osaka/Kyoto to Kanazawa**: "Take the Thunderbird Limited Express train to Tsuruga, then transfer to the Hokuriku Shinkansen. Total travel time is about 2 hours." Same source. JR West has a transfer guide for Tsuruga Station: https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/howto/tsuruga_change/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Sendai** (Tohoku Shinkansen): "Komachi, Hayabusa or Hayate ... just over an hour and a half. The slower Yamabiko trains take about two hours"; highway or night buses "about five to six hours". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tohoku/miyagi/, checked 24 Nov 2026. The Sendai page says highway buses take "between five and seven hours". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tohoku/miyagi/sendai-area/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Sapporo**: "Flights from Tokyo to Sapporo's New Chitose Airport take about 90 minutes. By train, it takes around eight hours from Tokyo. Take the JR Tohoku/Hokkaido Shinkansen from Tokyo to Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto, then transfer to the Hokuto limited express to Sapporo." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/hokkaido/, checked 24 Nov 2026. The Sapporo page gives the legs as Shinkansen to Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto "(4 hours)" and Hokuto to Sapporo "(3.5 hours)"; from New Chitose "a 35-minute express train ride to Sapporo Station". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/hokkaido/hokkaido/sapporo-and-around/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Tokyo to Fukuoka (Hakata)**: "about one hour and 45 minutes from Haneda Airport to Fukuoka Airport"; Tokyo Station to Hakata "via the JR Tokaido-Sanyo Shinkansen Line is about five hours". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/fukuoka/fukuoka-city/, checked 24 Nov 2026. The prefecture page says "two hours by plane or five hours by shinkansen from Tokyo". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/fukuoka/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Osaka to Fukuoka**: "The shinkansen ride from Shin-Osaka to Hakata Station is around two hours and 20 minutes"; "Flights from Osaka's Itami Airport to Fukuoka Airport take one hour and 15 minutes." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/fukuoka/fukuoka-city/, checked 24 Nov 2026. **Differs:** Osaka's bureau gives Hakata to Shin-Osaka "Shinkansen (2 hours 30 minutes)", Fukuoka to Itami or KIX by plane "1 hour 20 minutes", bus "approximately 9-10 hours". Source: https://osaka-info.jp/en/information/to-osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Kyoto to Fukuoka**: Hakata to Kyoto by Nozomi "takes less than 3 hours"; Hakata to Kyoto shown as "(Approx. 170 minutes)". Source: https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Osaka to Kyoto**: JR Special Rapid Service from Osaka Station "approximately 30 minutes"; Hankyu from Osaka-umeda to Kyoto-Kawaramachi "approximately 45 minutes". Source: https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Other Shinkansen times to Shin-Osaka (Osaka bureau): from Sendai "4 hours 30 minutes", Nagoya "1 hour", Kagoshima-Chuo "3 hours 50 minutes". Planes to Osaka: from New Chitose "1 hour 50 minutes" (Itami) or "2 hours" (KIX); from Sendai "1 hour 20 minutes"; from Naha "2 hours 10 minutes" (Itami) or "2 hours 20 minutes" (KIX). Source: https://osaka-info.jp/en/information/to-osaka/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Distances in km: only a few official figures were reachable. Narita Airport is "about 60 kilometers east of central Tokyo in Chiba Prefecture" (https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/narita-airport/); the Ogasawara Islands lie "1,000 kilometers south of Tokyo" (https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/weather/); JR West runs "Shinkansen: 937.7 km (2 line segments)" (https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/about-us/overview/). All checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Gap:** no reachable official page gives city-to-city km (e.g. Tokyo to Shin-Osaka, Tokyo to Hakata). JR Central (global.jr-central.co.jp) and JR East pages would be the source but are blocked. Answer "how far" with travel time and say the km is not on our sources.
- **Gap:** Tokyo to Kyoto or Kanazawa by plane: not stated on a reachable official page (Kyoto has no airport; nearest are Itami and Kansai, "between 1 hour and 1.5 hours away", https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kansai/kyoto/).

## 3. Main international airports

- Overview: "Japan's main airport hubs are situated just outside the major cities of Tokyo and Osaka"; "Multiple airports handle international flights, including those in Tokyo and Osaka as well as Naha and Fukuoka." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-to-japan/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Narita (NRT)**, Tokyo: "one of two airports serving Tokyo"; "about 60 kilometers east of central Tokyo in Chiba Prefecture, handles most of the city's international flights and some domestic flights". Narita Express to Tokyo Station "in just under an hour"; Keisei Skyliner to Nippori and Ueno "in around 40 minutes" (not covered by the JR Pass). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/narita-airport/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Official site: https://www.narita-airport.jp/en/ (reachable).
- **Haneda (HND)**, Tokyo: "also known as Tokyo International Airport, is the closest airport to Tokyo. It's around 30 minutes by train from the center of Tokyo"; three terminals, Terminal 3 is international; Keikyu to Shinagawa "around 20 minutes"; Tokyo Monorail to Hamamatsucho "in around 20 minutes"; Haneda to Narita by rail "around 1 hour 45 minutes". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/haneda-airport/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Official site: https://tokyo-haneda.com/en/index.html (reachable).
- **Kansai International (KIX)**, Osaka: "serves the western area of Japan housing ... Kyoto and ... Osaka, along with ... Nara and Wakayama"; "around 50 minutes from the bullet train hub of Shin-Osaka"; JR and rapid trains reach central Osaka "in just over 30 minutes". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/osaka-kix/, checked 24 Nov 2026. **Differs** on KIX to Kyoto: that page says "100 minutes away from Kyoto Station"; JNTO's Getting to Japan page says "75 minutes away from Kyoto Station"; Kyoto City says the Haruka limited express takes "approximately 80 minutes" direct (https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/). Official site: https://www.kansai-airport.or.jp/en/ (refused our sandbox with HTTP 403; not checked directly).
- **Chubu Centrair (NGO)**, Nagoya: "Central Japan International Airport, Nagoya—or Centrair—is an air transport hub for flights to and from Asian cities and airports throughout Japan." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Centrair to Kyoto "Approx. 70 min" (Meitetsu μSKY to Nagoya about 30 minutes, then Tokaido Shinkansen). Source: https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-to/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Official site: https://www.centrair.jp/en/ (blocked, not checked).
- **New Chitose (CTS)**, Hokkaido: "in the southwest of Hokkaido, close to Sapporo and the popular ski resorts of Niseko and Rusutsu ... the main connection between Hokkaido and the rest of Japan"; JR to Sapporo "just under 40 minutes"; bus "about 1 hour and 15 minutes"; "The airport opens at 5:00 a.m. and closes at 11 p.m." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/new-chitose-airport/, checked 24 Nov 2026. The airport itself says JR to Sapporo "takes about 30 minutes without transfers". Source: https://www.hokkaido-airports.com/en/new-chitose/access/jr/, checked 24 Nov 2026. (The old URL www.new-chitose-airport.jp/en/ redirects to https://www.hokkaido-airports.com/en/new-chitose/.)
- **Fukuoka (FUK)**, Kyushu: "a quick five-minute train ride from Hakata—the heart of Fukuoka City". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/, checked 24 Nov 2026. "the airport is only six minutes away by subway" from Hakata Station. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kyushu/fukuoka/fukuoka-city/, checked 24 Nov 2026. The subway station "is directly connected by escalator to the south side of the Domestic Terminal Building"; from the International Terminal take the free shuttle bus to the Domestic Terminal. Source: https://www.fukuoka-airport.jp/en/access/subway.html, checked 24 Nov 2026. Official site: https://www.fukuoka-airport.jp/en/ (reachable).
- **Naha (OKA)**, Okinawa: "Naha Airport is the gateway to all of the Okinawa islands"; Naha city is "a 15-minute drive from the airport or a few stops on the monorail". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/okinawa/okinawa/naha-and-the-main-island/, checked 24 Nov 2026. "If you are traveling to the subtropical islands of Okinawa from Japan's main cities you have to fly." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Official site: https://www.naha-airport.co.jp/en/ (blocked, not checked).
- "Most prefectures in Japan have at least one airport". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

## 4. Getting around

**Shinkansen**
- Three seat types: "Non-reserved seats, Reserved seats, Green Car (first class) seats"; Tohoku, Hokkaido, Hokuriku and Joetsu lines also have "GranClass". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/shinkansen/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- "Many Shinkansen have both reserved and non-reserved seating areas, although some services are reserved seating only." On the Tokaido-Sanyo line there are three train types, "Nozomi, Hikari, and Kodama"; "The Nozomi ... stops at fewer stations". Same source.
- Bags over 160 cm total on the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen: reserve a seat with oversized-baggage space ahead of time; "There's no extra charge for reserving this space". Same source.
- Wrong train: "move to the non-reserved seating area (if there is one) and inform the train staff". Eating and drinking "are generally allowed on Shinkansen trains". Same source.
- Fastest trains such as "the Hayabusa (Tohoku Shinkansen), and Kagayaki (Hokuriku Shinkansen) ... don't offer non-reserved seats, so seat reservations are mandatory". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/jr-rail-pass/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- In holiday seasons (Golden Week, summer, New Year) Shinkansen "are expected to be crowded and many of them will only offer reserved seating". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- JR West: a limited express ticket (reserved or non-reserved) "is required in addition to the basic fare ticket" for Shinkansen and limited expresses. Source: https://www.westjr.co.jp/travel-information/en/train-usage-guide/howto/basics/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Sanyo, Kyushu, Nishi Kyushu and Hokuriku Shinkansen seats can be booked online with JR West; "Reservations are accepted up to one month (same day of preceding month) in advance of departure, beginning at 10:00 am (JST)". Source: https://www.westjr.co.jp/travel-information/en/tickets-passes/route-search/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**IC cards (Suica, PASMO, ICOCA and others)**
- "Most regions have their own IC card ... These cards can be used nationwide"; most need a deposit; "Welcome Suica and TOURIST PASMO, are available without a deposit". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/ic-card/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Welcome Suica is "a deposit-free card for tourists at certain places in the Kanto area, which is valid for up to 28 days"; ICOCA and TOICA are used in other areas; some areas and "highway buses" do not take IC cards. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/how-to-travel-by-train-and-bus-in-japan/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- ICOCA (JR West) works in the Kitaca, PASMO, Suica, manaca, TOICA, PiTaPa, Hayakaken, nimoca and SUGOCA areas; "With some exceptions, ICOCA cannot be used for traveling between two different areas"; "ICOCA cannot be used on Shinkansen trains." Source: https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/howto/icoca/area/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- ICOCA can be added to Apple Pay on "iPhone 8 or later with iOS 16.0 or later". Source: https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/howto/icoca/applepay/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Mobile Suica/PASMO "may not work on all smartphones". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/how-to-travel-by-train-and-bus-in-japan/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Japan Rail Pass**
- "offered exclusively to foreign visitors traveling in Japan under temporary visitor status"; covers "JR train lines countrywide, with very few exceptions", most Shinkansen, rapid, express and local JR trains, some JR buses and the JR West Miyajima ferry. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/jr-rail-pass/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Not covered: Nozomi and Mizuho (need an extra ticket), subways and private lines, some "liner" trains. Same source; also https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/shinkansen/.
- "As of October 1, 2026, the Japan Rail Pass can be purchased either through the official website or through an authorized overseas distributor." Seat reservations on most Shinkansen are free with the pass. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/jr-rail-pass/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Official site: https://japanrailpass.net/en/ (blocked, not checked).
- It is "jointly offered by six JR Group companies"; regional passes (JR EAST PASS, JR-West Rail Passes, Hokkaido Rail Pass, ALL SHIKOKU, JR KYUSHU RAIL PASS and others) may suit one area better. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/jr-rail-passes/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Other trains and subways**
- Subways: "your JR Rail Pass is not valid. However, it is possible to use your Suica Card"; if unsure of the fare "purchase the cheapest one and adjust the fare when you exit". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/subways/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Route and fare tools named by JNTO: Japan Transit Planner (Jorudan) and NAVITIME. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/traveling-by-rail/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Domestic flights**
- "With some 90 airports serving domestic and overseas flights, air travel within Japan is very convenient"; JAL and ANA offer visitor fares; LCCs restrict luggage; flights "can be delayed by typhoons and even volcanic activity". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/domestic-air-travel/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Highway buses**
- "generally less expensive than traveling by airplane or railway and run both day and night"; "the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal is a hub for country-wide travel"; "weather and traffic can cause significant delays". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/buses/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Taxis**
- "fares are calculated by the running meter"; "A late-night surcharge is added between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m."; red sign means available, green occupied; "Doors open and close automatically"; drivers "do not expect or accept tips"; cards generally accepted in big-city taxis but "cash might be your only option in countryside cabs". Narita and Haneda have flat-rate fares to central Tokyo. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/taxis/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Driving**
- "Cars drive on the left side of the road"; a booklet-type paper International Driving Permit is valid, card or smartphone IDPs are not. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/getting-around/cars/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Licences from France, Switzerland, Germany, Belgium, Monaco and Taiwan need a Japanese translation instead of an IDP. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

- Visit Japan Web: "Registration is not required, but advance registration makes procedures like tax-free shopping more convenient" (https://services.digital.go.jp/en/visit-japan-web/). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/visa-info/, checked 24 Nov 2026. It lets you "pre-register necessary information for arrival procedures 'Immigration,' 'Customs,' and 'Tax-Free Shopping' online" and generate a QR code. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/customs-and-duty/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Customs: a Customs Declaration is handed in on arrival (paper or "e-filing via web page or QR code"); "Bringing meat, meat products, plants and plant products into Japan is strictly prohibited." Official page: https://www.customs.go.jp/english/summary/passenger.htm. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/customs-and-duty/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- A customs declaration is required when carrying "more than one million yen in cash, checks, traveler's checks, securities, etc." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/currency/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Drugs: "cannabis is treated in the same way as other drugs ... please don't bring cannabis into the country; even if it is prescribed in your country of origin." Carry your passport "at all times". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/local-laws/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Medicines: JNTO points to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026. **Gap:** the MHLW page was not fetched.
- A departure tax (International Tourist Tax) is charged on leaving Japan; see https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/international-tourist-tax/ (fee not repeated here), checked 24 Nov 2026.

## 6. Money, phones, power, tax-free, emergencies, tipping

**Money and ATMs**
- "In Japan, there are still many places where cash is required"; "many places, especially in rural areas, may still only accept cash, even hotels"; most widely accepted cards are "Visa, JCB and Mastercard". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/cashless-payments-in-japan/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Overseas phone wallets "such as ApplePay, GooglePay and AliPay sometimes cannot be used in Japan". Same source.
- "You can withdraw cash at ATMs in Japan Post Bank and in Seven Bank." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/credit-cards/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- ATMs for foreign cards (JNTO list): Japan Post Bank (mostly in post offices, also some stations and supermarkets; English available); Seven Bank (mainly in 7-Eleven stores, 12 languages for overseas cards); Mizuho Bank; Mitsubishi UFJ Bank; SMBC. Narita, Haneda, Centrair and Kansai airports have ATMs. "ATMs often charge for withdrawals"; "Not all ATMs are 24 hours". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/currency/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- "Yen (JPY) comes in bills of 1,000 yen, 2,000 yen, 5,000 yen and 10,000 yen"; the page lists coins of 1, 5, 10, 50 and 500 yen (it omits the 100-yen coin, which JNTO's phone page mentions: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/telephone/). Same source.
- "Japanese public transport, stores and restaurants are legally forbidden to accept foreign currencies". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/airport-access/osaka-kix/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Mobile and Wi-Fi**
- Major international airports "provide free Wi-Fi within their terminals"; pocket Wi-Fi rental kiosks "are located at all major airports"; SIM cards sold at airports and electronics stores. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/wifi-and-connectivity/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- "For short-term visitors, eSIMs and pocket Wi-Fi devices are especially convenient." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Free numbers starting 0120 "cannot be called from outside Japan". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/telephone/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Power**
- "Japan's standard voltage is 100V ... 50Hz or 60Hz, depending on the region. Power outlets use Type A plugs." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- "50 Hertz in eastern Japan and 60 Hertz in western Japan (including Nagoya, Kyoto and Osaka)"; dual-voltage devices work, others need a step-down transformer. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/plug-and-electricity/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Tax-free shopping**
- "tax exemption is available only under certain conditions". Official page: https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/tax-free/page01_000113.html. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/japans-tax-exemption/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Change: "For purchases made on or after November 1, 2026, the revised system adopts a 'refund method,' under which the consumption tax equivalent is refunded after Customs confirms at departure that the goods are being taken out of Japan." Since 1 April 2025, items the buyer posts overseas are not tax-free. Same source. (Refund-method details: https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/tax-free/page01_000001_00028.html, blocked here.)
- Bring your passport; "You must have an entry stamp to be eligible for tax exemption. When using the automatic gates, make sure to get an entry stamp from a nearby immigration officer." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Note: the FAQ's "procedures must be done at the time of purchase" text predates the 1 Nov 2026 refund method; prefer the Tax Exemption page.

**Emergencies**
- Police 110; Fire/Ambulance 119. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/hotline/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- JNTO Japan Visitor Hotline: 050-3816-2787 (from overseas +81-50-3816-2787), "24 hours a day, 365 days a year", English, Chinese, Korean; for "emergencies (accident, illness etc.) or natural disasters, general tourist information". Same source.
- Tokyo Metropolitan Police English helpline 03-3501-0110, 24 hours. For petty crime, go to a koban (police box); a police report is needed for passport reissue or insurance. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/emergencies/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Disaster info: JNTO points to Japan Safe Travel Information, the Safety Tips app and NHK WORLD-JAPAN. Same source.
- "Japan is considered one of the safest countries in the world." Tap water "is safe to drink". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

**Tipping**
- "In Japan, it's not common to tip for services such as those provided in bars, cafes, restaurants, taxis, and hotels." If tipping a private guide, "it is customary to put it in an envelope". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/tipping-in-japan/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- "No. Japan does not have a tipping culture." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.

## 7. Seasons and weather

- Seasons (Tokyo averages, JNTO FAQ): Spring (Mar–May) about 10–20°C; Summer (Jun–Aug) about 25–35°C with high humidity; Autumn (Sep–Nov) about 15–25°C; Winter (Dec–Feb) about 0–10°C. "Hokkaido ... experiences heavy snowfall in winter"; "Okinawa ... has a warm climate throughout the year". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Cherry blossoms: "Starting in the south and gradually moving north, the blossoms bloom between March and May depending on your location." Rainy season: "the rainy season begins in May to June." Typhoons: "August and September may bring typhoons". Autumn leaves: from Hokkaido south "between late September and early December". Winter: "the northern areas are covered with Japan's peerless powder snow". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/weather/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Sakura: "Sakura season begins on the island of Kyushu, southwestern Japan, in March"; late viewing "in mountain areas, as well as in Tohoku and Hokkaido". Average first-bloom dates (JNTO table, from the Japan Weather Association): Kochi 22 Mar; Fukuoka 22 Mar; Tokyo (Chiyoda) 24 Mar; Nagoya 24 Mar; Hiroshima 25 Mar; Kagoshima 26 Mar; Osaka 27 Mar; Kanazawa 3 Apr; Sendai 8 Apr; Niigata 8 Apr; Nagano 11 Apr; Akita 17 Apr; Aomori 22 Apr; Sapporo 1 May; Kushiro 16 May. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/see-and-do/cherry-blossom-forecast/, checked 24 Nov 2026. The page's own forecast is for 2026 (already past); use the averages and say "the forecast is subject to change".
- Golden Week ("The end of April") is a peak travel period; hay fever in spring. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/spring-guide/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Peak seasons: New Year (late Dec to early Jan), Golden Week (late Apr to early May), summer (mid-July to August, esp. Obon in mid-August). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Okinawa: "Travel plans should be made cautiously during the rainy season from late May to early June, and in September when typhoons frequently hit islands." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/okinawa/okinawa/naha-and-the-main-island/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Tokyo: "hot and humid summers punctuated with typhoons ... cold, dry winters. Snow is rare". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kanto/tokyo/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Winter snow regions: "while it rarely snows in most of the country's metropolitan areas, the surrounding mountains and northern regions are regularly covered"; skiing in Nagano (Hakuba) and Hokkaido (Niseko, Rusutsu); "During the New Year's holidays, most restaurants and attractions shut down." Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/winter-guide/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Niigata and Nagano "offer some of the best skiing and snowboarding in Japan". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/hokuriku-shinetsu/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Mt. Fuji climbing season "typically runs from early July to mid-September"; climbing outside it is prohibited. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Earthquakes and volcanoes: "Japan has over 100 active volcanos"; "Small earthquakes happen on a daily basis". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/weather/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- **Gap:** JMA (jma.go.jp) pages on rainy-season onset by region, typhoon climatology and snowfall were blocked. Regional rainy-season dates for mainland Japan are only "May to June" (JNTO).

## 8. Etiquette

- Trains: speaking on the phone "is generally frowned upon while riding trains and buses"; "line up to the side of the train to let passengers disembark before boarding"; give priority seats to "the elderly, pregnant or disabled". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/japanese-manners-dos-and-donts/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- On the Shinkansen "it is okay to use the corridor between cars" for calls; take backpacks off; no smoking or vaping; women-only cars in the morning rush. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/how-to-travel-by-train-and-bus-in-japan/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Eating: fine on Shinkansen and limited expresses, but "eating on local or subway trains is generally considered impolite". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/traveling-by-rail/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- JR West: "please form lines when waiting for the train"; "set your cell phone to silent mode"; do not leave suitcases by the doors. Source: https://www.westjr.co.jp/travel-information/en/train-usage-guide/howto/manners/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Shoes: "take off your shoes at the entrance area inside the doorway and slip on the indoor-use slippers ... remove your slippers when on the tatami mats"; separate toilet slippers. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/understanding-and-mastering-japanese-manners-and-etiquette/, checked 24 Nov 2026. Homes, "Japanese-style restaurants, temples, hot springs etc." may have no-shoe policies. Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/japanese-manners-dos-and-donts/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Onsen: "remove all clothing before entering"; "Wash your body with soap before entering the baths"; "Do not put your towel or other items in the water"; tie up hair. Tattoos "are generally not permitted in hot springs, public bathhouses, swimming pools, or gyms" with exceptions; check ahead or use a private bath (kashikiriburo). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/understanding-and-mastering-japanese-manners-and-etiquette/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Smoking: "Smoking while walking on the street or throwing your butts on the ground is prohibited and may incur a fine." Same source. Kyoto fines smoking outside designated areas in the centre (Kiyomizu, Gion, around Kyoto Station). Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kansai/kyoto/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Dining: slurping noodles is accepted; do not dip sushi rice in soy sauce; leaving food "is frowned upon". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/japanese-manners-dos-and-donts/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- General: "Public trash cans can be limited, so you may need to carry your trash"; ask before photographing people; at temples and shrines avoid "overly revealing clothing". Source: https://www.japan.travel/en/faq/, checked 24 Nov 2026.
- Japan Tourism Agency guide "Travel Etiquette for the future": https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/responsible-traveler/en.html (linked from JNTO; blocked here).
