Narita is the main airport for many visitors to Tokyo, but it is big, spread over three terminals, and far from the city. This guide is written in plain English so that a first-time visitor, a family or an older traveller can follow it step by step. You can print the one-page card below and carry it with you.

Narita on one page

  • Where: Narita is in Chiba, about an hour or more from central Tokyo.
  • Terminals: there are 3. Terminals 1 and 2 each have a train station. Terminal 3 does not: walk to Terminal 2 (about 15 minutes) or take the free shuttle bus.
  • Before you fly: register on Visit Japan Web (free) and keep the QR codes on your phone.
  • After landing: immigration, then baggage, then customs, then the arrivals hall.
  • In the arrivals hall: SIM and Wi-Fi counters, cash machines, convenience stores and train tickets.
  • Free Wi-Fi: connect to FreeWiFi-NARITA for quick messages.
  • Need help? Every terminal has information counters. Staff will help, so just ask.

Which terminal?

Narita has three terminals, and they are not next to each other. Check your ticket or airline app, because airlines can change terminals.

Moving between terminals: a free shuttle bus links the terminals and takes about 10 minutes by road. Between Terminal 2 and Terminal 3 you can also take a 3-minute shuttle or walk for about 15 minutes.

Your first hour after landing

Most people follow the same four steps. Signs are in Japanese and English, and staff are nearby if you are unsure.

  1. Before you fly (optional but helpful). Register on the free Visit Japan Web site. You enter your immigration and customs details once and get QR codes. Take a screenshot of them in case your signal is weak.
  2. Immigration. Have your passport ready. Narita's Terminals 1 and 2 have added self-service kiosks that handle immigration and customs together for foreign visitors (reported as fully running from 24 September 2026). Follow the signs and staff, and ask if you are unsure.
  3. Baggage claim. Find your flight on the screens and collect your bags.
  4. Customs. Show your Visit Japan Web QR code, or a paper declaration form (many flights hand these out).

Then you arrive in the arrivals hall. At Terminals 1 and 2 you will find convenience stores, coffee shops, currency exchange, and SIM and pocket Wi-Fi counters. At Terminal 3 these services are on the 2nd floor. Train tickets are sold at the arrivals halls of Terminals 1 and 2.

Rules change. Immigration and customs procedures are updated often. If anything here differs from what the signs or officers tell you, follow the signs and officers.

Two trains look alike. The Skyliner and the Access Express can leave from the same platform. Check the train name and departure time on the display before you board.

Late arrival? The Narita Express and Skyliner are reported to stop around 11 pm, while buses run later. If you land late, check the last train before you book.

Going on to Haneda? Keisei runs a direct train service to Haneda Airport (about 2 hours by Access Express), and a limousine bus takes about 85 minutes.

Baby and nursing rooms

Baby rooms are spread across the floors of the terminals. They are described as having a breast-feeding booth, a nappy-changing counter, a wash basin and a hot water dispenser. The airport's pages we checked do not give a floor-by-floor list, so ask at an information counter for the nearest one.

If you need help

Every terminal has information counters, and staff there speak several languages. If you are tired, unsure or carrying a lot, go straight to a counter instead of searching on your own.

Sources: Narita Airport's own pages on facility layout, quiet rooms and Kids Park. Floors and opening hours can change, so confirm on the day. Found something out of date? Tell us.

Leaving Narita

  • Know your terminal. The stations are different: Terminal 1 has its own, while Terminals 2 and 3 share one. Check before you set off.
  • Terminal 3 takes longer. If you arrive by train, you get off at Terminal 2 and walk about 15 minutes or take the shuttle. Allow extra time.
  • Return rented gear first. If you rented a pocket Wi-Fi, hand it back before you reach the departures lobby, especially at Terminal 1.
  • Allow time. Check-in, security and passport control all take time, so arrive early.

A short stop or a long layover

If you have less than about 4 hours between flights, stay in the airport. Immigration, the train ride and the trip back eat up the time. With a longer wait you could visit Naritasan Shinjoji Temple, which is a short trip from the airport, or rest in a day room. Narita also has paid shower rooms and day rooms for a break between flights.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Narita Airport in Tokyo?

No. Narita is in Chiba Prefecture, east of Tokyo. Plan on about an hour or more to reach central Tokyo.

Which terminal will my flight use?

It depends on your airline, so check your ticket or airline app. As a general guide, Terminal 1 North Wing is used by SkyTeam airlines, Terminal 1 South Wing by Star Alliance airlines, Terminal 2 by oneworld airlines and others, and Terminal 3 by low-cost airlines.

Is there free Wi-Fi at Narita?

Yes. Connect to FreeWiFi-NARITA. It works for quick messages, but it is not meant for a full day of maps and apps.

Do I need Visit Japan Web?

It is not required, because you can still use paper forms. But registering on the free Visit Japan Web site before you fly lets you show QR codes for immigration and customs, which can speed up your arrival.

How do I get from Narita to central Tokyo?

By train (Keisei Skyliner, JR Narita Express or the cheaper Keisei Access Express), by bus (the budget TYO-NRT bus or the Airport Limousine Bus), by taxi, or by a pre-booked private transfer. Trains are usually the quickest. Fares and times change, so check the operator before you go.

Is there a prayer room at Narita Airport?

Yes, in every terminal. Narita's facility list shows one on the 3rd floor of Terminal 1, on the 1st and 3rd floors of the Terminal 2 main building, and on the 2nd floor of the Terminal 3 main building. If you cannot find it, ask at an information counter.

Where can I find a baby room or a quiet room?

Baby rooms with a feeding booth, a nappy-changing counter, a sink and hot water are spread across the floors of the terminals. Narita also has small quiet rooms in each terminal. Ask at an information counter for the nearest one.

Can I walk between Terminal 2 and Terminal 3?

Yes. It is about a 15-minute walk along a covered walkway, or a short shuttle bus ride of about 3 minutes. Terminal 3 has no train station of its own, so rail passengers use Terminal 2 station.
