Narita on one page

  • Where: Narita is in Chiba, about an hour or more from central Tokyo.
  • Terminals: there are 3. Terminals 1 and 2 each have a train station. Terminal 3 does not: walk to Terminal 2 (about 15 minutes) or take the free shuttle bus.
  • Before you fly: register on Visit Japan Web (free) and keep the QR codes on your phone. Every traveller, children included, needs their own QR code.
  • After landing: immigration, then baggage, then customs, then the arrivals hall.
  • In the arrivals hall: SIM and Wi-Fi counters, cash machines, convenience stores and train tickets.
  • Free Wi-Fi: connect to FreeWiFi-NARITA for quick messages.
  • Need help? Every terminal has information counters. Staff will help, so just ask.

Which terminal?

Narita has three terminals, and they are not next to each other. Check your ticket or airline app, because airlines can change terminals.

TerminalWho uses itTrain station
Terminal 1Most international airlines. North Wing: SkyTeam airlines. South Wing: Star Alliance airlines, including ANA.Narita Airport Station (basement)
Terminal 2oneworld airlines and others.Airport Terminal 2·3 Station
Terminal 3Low-cost airlines.None. Use Terminal 2 station and walk or take the shuttle.
Moving between terminals: a free shuttle bus links the terminals and takes about 10 minutes by road. Between Terminal 2 and Terminal 3 you can also take a 3-minute shuttle or walk for about 15 minutes.

Your first hour after landing

Most people follow the same four steps. Signs are in Japanese and English, and staff are nearby if you are unsure.

  1. Before you fly (optional but helpful). Register on the free Visit Japan Web site. You enter your immigration and customs details once and get QR codes. Take a screenshot of them in case your signal is weak.
  2. Immigration. Have your passport ready. Narita has self-service kiosks that handle immigration and customs together for foreign visitors: Terminal 3 since April 2025, and Terminals 1 and 2 from 24 September 2026. Follow the signs and staff, and ask if you are unsure.
  3. Baggage claim. Find your flight on the screens and collect your bags.
  4. Customs. Show your Visit Japan Web QR code, or a paper declaration form (many flights hand these out).

Then you arrive in the arrivals hall. At Terminals 1 and 2 you will find convenience stores, coffee shops, currency exchange, and SIM and pocket Wi-Fi counters. At Terminal 3 these services are on the 2nd floor. Train tickets are sold at the arrivals halls of Terminals 1 and 2.

Rules change. Immigration and customs procedures are updated often. If anything here differs from what the signs or officers tell you, follow the signs and officers.

A train card (Suica or PASMO)

A tap-on travel card saves you buying a ticket for every train, subway or bus ride, and it also pays in many shops and vending machines. Visitors usually buy a tourist version, called Welcome Suica. It has no deposit, it is valid for 28 days, and any money left on it cannot be refunded.

  • Where at Narita: JR East Travel Service Centers and dedicated Welcome Suica ticket machines at Narita Airport Station (Terminal 1) and Airport Terminal 2·3 Station.
  • Queues: expect a line just after a busy arrival. If the machine queue is long, try the service counter.
  • iPhone users: an app version, Welcome Suica Mobile, lets you set up before you land (iPhone only, as of April 2026).
  • Staying longer than 28 days? A regular Suica or PASMO may suit you better. Ask at the counter.
This changes often. Sales points and card types have changed several times in recent years. Check JR East's Welcome Suica page before you travel.

Two trains look alike. The Skyliner and the Access Express can leave from the same platform. Check the train name and departure time on the display before you board.

Late arrival? The last Narita Express leaves Terminal 1 at 21:44 on weekdays (JR East timetable, December 2026 issue). Keisei trains and some buses leave later than that, but times differ by operator and by day, so check the timetable before you rely on one. If you land after about 9 pm, a fixed-fare taxi or a pre-booked transfer is the fallback.

Going on to Haneda? Keisei runs a direct train service to Haneda Airport (about 2 hours by Access Express), and a limousine bus takes about 85 minutes.

Sending your suitcase to your hotel

If you do not want to carry heavy bags on a crowded train, you can post them ahead. Two companies, JAL ABC and Yamato, run delivery counters at Narita. Ask which counter is in your terminal.

  • Usually next day. JAL ABC says the earliest delivery from the airport is the next day. Some same-day services exist for limited areas, with cut-off times, so do not plan around same-day.
  • Prices vary by size. JAL ABC lists ¥1,950 for a bag up to 120 cm (length, width and height added together) sent to Tokyo, Kanagawa, Chiba or Saitama. Bigger bags cost more.
  • Have these ready: your hotel name, address and phone number, written down.
  • Keep with you: medicines, valuables, chargers and a change of clothes.

Sources: Narita Airport: baggage delivery and JAL ABC airport luggage delivery, checked 18 November 2026. Prices and cut-off times change, so confirm at the counter.

Baby and nursing rooms

Baby rooms are spread across the floors of the terminals. They are described as having a breast-feeding booth, a nappy-changing counter, a wash basin and a hot water dispenser. The airport's pages we checked do not give a floor-by-floor list, so ask at an information counter for the nearest one.

If you need help

Every terminal has information counters, and staff there speak several languages. If you are tired, unsure or carrying a lot, go straight to a counter instead of searching on your own.

Sources: Narita Airport's own pages on facility layout, quiet rooms and Kids Park. Floors and opening hours can change, so confirm on the day. Found something out of date? Tell us.

Help for older travellers and anyone who needs assistance

You do not have to cope alone. Narita is set up to help, and asking early is easier than struggling.

  • Information counters. Look for the "?" sign. Staff can answer questions and solve problems.
  • Phone help. Narita Airport Information is open 24 hours a day: +81 476-34-8000.
  • Facilities. Narita lists accessible toilets, wheelchair rental, special-assistance and intercom services, and quiet rooms in all three terminals. Lifts have voice guidance, low buttons and Braille, and the terminal shuttle buses have low floors for wheelchairs.
  • Trains. Reserved trains such as the Narita Express may have only one or two wheelchair spaces, so book early and tell station staff you need help.
  • Before you fly. Ask your airline to arrange assistance at the airport when you book.

Sources: Narita Airport: customers requiring assistance, universal design facilities and other facilities; Japan National Tourism Organization.

Leaving Narita

  • Know your terminal. The stations are different: Terminal 1 has its own, while Terminals 2 and 3 share one. Check before you set off.
  • Terminal 3 takes longer. If you arrive by train, you get off at Terminal 2 and walk about 15 minutes or take the shuttle. Allow extra time.
  • Return rented gear first. If you rented a pocket Wi-Fi, return it before you go through security: post it in the envelope it comes with, or use your provider's return counter.
  • Allow time. Check-in, security and passport control all take time, so arrive early.
  • Shopped tax-free? Since 1 November 2026 you pay the tax at the shop and claim it back at the airport. Within 90 days of buying, scan your passport at a tax-free kiosk in the international departure lobby before you check in bags, with your receipts and the items with you. At Narita you can also start online with Visit Japan Web. Allow extra time, as queues are likely while the system is new. See the Japan National Tourism Organization guide (checked 16 November 2026).

A short stop or a long layover

If you have less than about 4 hours between flights, stay in the airport. Immigration, the train ride and the trip back eat up the time. With a longer wait you could visit Naritasan Shinjoji Temple, which is a short trip from the airport, or rest in a day room. Narita also has paid shower rooms and day rooms for a break between flights.

If you would rather not plan it yourself, You can book a private half-day Narita layover tour that visits the Naritasan approach street and the temple, with free cancellation up to 24 hours before. It has very few reviews so far, so read the listing first. Leaving the airport means entering Japan, so you need the visa or visa exemption that applies to your passport. Check with your airline or the Japanese embassy before you book, and leave plenty of time to get back. We earn a small commission if you book through this link, at no extra cost to you.

Landing late or leaving very early?

The 9h nine hours Narita Airport capsule hotel sits inside the airport area next to Terminal 2·3 Station and has a 24-hour front desk. Its own site lists check-in from 2 pm to 5 am (tell them in advance if you will arrive after midnight) and check-out by 10 am. From Terminal 1 or Terminal 3, use the terminal shuttle bus. The Japan National Tourism Organization lists it as the closest hotel for international flights.

🏨 Good for: one traveller landing late or flying early, with a cabin-size bag. It is a capsule hotel: one guest per capsule, guests aged 6 and over, and no room to keep large suitcases (use the airport’s baggage storage). See rooms at 9h nine hours Narita Airport (partner link).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Narita Airport in Tokyo?

No. Narita is in Chiba Prefecture, east of Tokyo. Plan on about an hour or more to reach central Tokyo.

Which terminal will my flight use?

It depends on your airline, so check your ticket or airline app. As a general guide, Terminal 1 North Wing is used by SkyTeam airlines, Terminal 1 South Wing by Star Alliance airlines, Terminal 2 by oneworld airlines and others, and Terminal 3 by low-cost airlines.

Is there free Wi-Fi at Narita?

Yes. Connect to FreeWiFi-NARITA. It works for quick messages, but it is not meant for a full day of maps and apps.

Do I need Visit Japan Web?

It is not required, because you can still use paper forms. But registering on the free Visit Japan Web site before you fly lets you show QR codes for immigration and customs, which can speed up your arrival.

How do I get from Narita to central Tokyo?

By train (Keisei Skyliner, JR Narita Express or the cheaper Keisei Access Express), by bus (the budget TYO-NRT bus or the Airport Limousine Bus), by taxi, or by a pre-booked private transfer. Trains are usually the quickest. Fares and times change, so check the operator before you go.

Is there a prayer room at Narita Airport?

Yes, in every terminal. Narita's facility list shows one on the 3rd floor of Terminal 1, on the 1st and 3rd floors of the Terminal 2 main building, and on the 2nd floor of the Terminal 3 main building. If you cannot find it, ask at an information counter.

Where can I find a baby room or a quiet room?

Baby rooms with a feeding booth, a nappy-changing counter, a sink and hot water are spread across the floors of the terminals. Narita also has small quiet rooms in each terminal. Ask at an information counter for the nearest one.

Can I walk between Terminal 2 and Terminal 3?

Yes. It is about a 15-minute walk along a covered walkway, or a short shuttle bus ride of about 3 minutes. Terminal 3 has no train station of its own, so rail passengers use Terminal 2 station.
