Shinjuku Station has gates on two levels and several railway companies, and it is easy to come out on the wrong side. The Narita Express, the airport buses, the Odakyū and Keiō lines and the Metro all use different parts of it. This guide is written in plain English so that a first-time visitor, a family or an older traveller can follow it step by step. You can print the one-page card below and carry it with you.

Shinjuku Station on one page

  • Last Narita Express: 19:39 from Shinjuku, every day (first 5:55), platforms 5 and 6. About 80 minutes to Narita Terminal 1 on a sample train (JR East). Timetables change, so check.
  • Airport buses: Busta Shinjuku, 4th floor. Use the JR New South Gate (2F, open 5:45 to 24:00), or the South Gate outside those hours. Narita: adult ¥3,600. Check the live timetable for the last bus.
  • Two sides: the West side has the Odakyū, Keiō and Toei lines and the West exit buses. The East side has Kabukichō and Seibu-Shinjuku. The South gates on 2F lead to Busta and the JR EAST Travel Service Center.
  • Between East and West: the B1 Free Passage, outside the ticket gates.
  • JR platforms: Yamanote Line 14 and 15, Chūō Line (Rapid) 7–8 and 11–12, Chūō limited express 9–10, Saikyō and Shōnan-Shinjuku lines 1–4.
  • Metro Marunouchi Line: many exits are closed during construction. Follow the signs.
  • Money: Seven Bank ATM in the Busta waiting room, 3:30 to 25:00 (1 am). Seven Bank takes cards issued outside Japan.
  • Luggage: Busta 4F coin lockers (up to 72 hours), or the Tokyo Tourist Information Center on Busta 3F (6:30 to 21:00).
  • Need help? JR EAST Travel Service Center, right after the New South Gate, 8:00 to 19:00. In an emergency, call 119.

Which side: West, or East and South?

Shinjuku Station has gates on two levels. On B1 are the East Gate, the West Gate, the Central East Gate and the Central West Gate (6:00 to the last train, also called the Keiō exit). On the 2nd floor are the South Gate, the Southeast Gate (7:00 to 24:00), the Kōshū-kaidō Gate, the New South Gate and the Miraina Tower Gate, according to JR East's station map.

In this guide we use two sides. The West side is for the Odakyū, Keiō and Toei lines and the West exit bus stop. The East and South side covers the East Gate (Kabukichō, Seibu-Shinjuku) and the 2nd-floor south gates, which lead to Busta Shinjuku and the JR EAST Travel Service Center. Outside the ticket gates, the B1 Free Passage links the East and West sides.

Narita Express and airport buses

The last Narita Express from Shinjuku leaves at 19:39, on weekdays and on Saturdays and holidays (JR East timetable, edition 2612). The first leaves at 5:55. After 19:39, plan another way to the airport.
  • Narita Express (N’EX). Platforms 5 and 6 (JR East's map). Trains run to Narita Airport Terminal 1, via Terminal 2·3. On weekdays they leave at 5:55, 6:34, 7:07, 7:27, 8:05, 9:05 and 9:40, then at about :08 and :38 past each hour until 16:38, then 17:09, 17:39, 18:09, 18:39 and 19:39. One sample train leaves at 10:08 and reaches Terminal 1 at 11:27, about 80 minutes.
  • Airport Limousine Bus to Narita. From the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal (Busta Shinjuku), 4th floor. Adult ¥3,600, child ¥1,800. The first sample bus leaves Busta at 5:40 and reaches Terminal 1 at 7:10, about 90 minutes.
  • Airport Limousine Bus to Haneda. Also from Busta. The bus company lists an early-morning bus at 3:50. Late-night and early-morning fares are higher: the route page lists adult ¥1,400 by day and ¥2,800 late at night.
  • Last buses. We did not verify the last buses to Narita or Haneda. Check the Airport Limousine Bus timetable on the day.
  • Bus tickets at Busta. The staffed Airport Limousine ticket office is open 4:20 to 22:50, and the ticket machines 3:40 to 22:50.
  • Getting to Busta. Through the JR New South Gate (2F), open 5:45 to 24:00. Busta says it is about 2 minutes from there. Outside those hours, use the South Gate (about 4 minutes).

Odakyū, Keiō, Seibu, Metro and Toei

Several railway companies meet at Shinjuku. JR East's station map shows how to reach them from the JR gates.

  • Odakyū Line (West side). Reached on B1 from the West side. The 2F South Gate is also marked for the Odakyū Line. The Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center is at the B1 West Underground Exit.
  • Keiō Line (West side). Through the Central West Gate, also called the Keiō exit, open from 6:00 to the last train.
  • Toei Shinjuku and Ōedo lines (West side). On B1 via the Central West Gate area. Tokyo Metro's map also shows a route to the Ōedo Line at Shinjuku-nishiguchi (E01, B4F).
  • Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line (M08). Platforms on B2F, reached via the East side, or by the lift at the West exit bus stop. Tokyo Metro's map (as of November 2025) marks many exits as closed during construction, for example A11, A13, A14, A15, A18, B9, B13, B14 and B15. Follow the signs.
  • Seibu-Shinjuku Station. GO TOKYO's map puts Kabukichō Tower 1 minute from Seibu-Shinjuku Station, and Tokyo Metro's map shows a route to the Seibu Shinjuku Line near exit B10.
  • Buses. Expressway and airport buses leave from Busta Shinjuku (4F). There is also a West exit bus stop.

Routes change during construction, so follow the signs.

Money and ATMs

  • Seven Bank ATM, Busta Shinjuku. In the waiting room, 3:30 to 25:00 (1 am). Seven Bank says its ATMs accept cards issued outside Japan (Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, American Express, JCB, Discover and Diners).
  • ATMs in the Metro station. Tokyo Metro's station map marks ATMs on B1F and B2F.
  • Currency exchange, JR EAST Travel Service Center. Travelex, foreign currency to yen and back, 8:00 to 19:00. The center is on the right after the New South Ticket Gate (2F).
  • Currency exchange, Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center. 8:00 to 16:00, at the Odakyū B1 West Underground Exit.
  • Paying by card. The JR EAST Travel Service Center accepts JCB, Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Diners Club and Discover.

Hours change, so check before you go.

Lockers and luggage

Sort your bags out before you start walking. You have three choices: a coin locker, a storage counter, or sending the bag on.

  • Busta Shinjuku coin lockers (4th floor). Up to 72 hours. Small lockers cost ¥100 per 60 minutes (then ¥500 per 6 hours), medium ¥100 per 40 minutes (then ¥600 per 6 hours) and large ¥100 per 30 minutes (then ¥900 per 6 hours). They can be used from 30 minutes before the first bus until the last bus. Keep the receipt: you need its QR code to open the locker.
  • Tokyo Tourist Information Center, Busta 3rd floor. 6:30 to 21:00, seven days a week. GO TOKYO says it offers delivery of parcels and temporary safekeeping of personal belongings (only at Busta Shinjuku).
  • Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center (West side). Baggage storage from 8:00 to 16:00, with pick-up until 19:00. It is at the Odakyū Line B1, West Underground Exit.
  • JR coin lockers. JR East's station map shows several coin lockers inside the station. We did not find their sizes or prices, so we give none.
  • JR EAST Travel Service Center. Its page does not list luggage storage or delivery.
  • Keep with you: medicines, valuables, chargers and a change of clothes.
  • Have these ready: your hotel name, address and phone number, written down.

Prices and hours change, so check at the counter.

Help for older travellers and anyone who needs assistance

You do not have to cope alone. Asking early is easier than struggling.

  • English information (JR East). 2F South Gate: 10:00 to 18:00 inside the ticket gates, 10:00 to 17:00 outside. B1 West Gate, inside the gates: 10:00 to 17:00.
  • JR EAST Travel Service Center. On the right after the New South Ticket Gate (2F), 8:00 to 19:00, open all year. Tourist information for the East Japan and Hokuriku areas, and maps of the Shinjuku area.
  • Busta Shinjuku information counter. 7:00 to 23:00. Busta says it supports English and Japanese at all hours.
  • Station staff. Tell staff at a ticket gate or ticket office if you need help or are not sure which way to go.
  • Lifts. JR East's map shows lifts lettered A to O to the platforms, a lift to the ground and to the Marunouchi Line (B2) at the West exit bus stop, and a lift to the Kōshū-kaidō overpass near the Miraina Tower Gate. Busta has wheelchair-accessible toilets.
  • Allow extra time. We did not find official walking times between gates and platforms, so add a buffer, especially with luggage.
  • Visitor hotline. The Japan Visitor Hotline, 050-3816-2787, is open 24 hours and helps in English, Chinese and Korean. In an emergency, call 119.

Day trips from Shinjuku

  • Hakone by Romancecar (Odakyū). Odakyū says the Romancecar limited express runs direct from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto in about 80 minutes. All seats are reserved, and you need a limited express ticket as well as a regular fare ticket. The Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center (B1, West Underground Exit, 8:00 to 16:00) sells Romancecar tickets and the Hakone Freepass, Hakone-Kamakura Pass and Fuji Hakone Pass.
  • Kōfu and Matsumoto (JR). The Chūō Line limited express uses platforms 9 and 10 (JR East's map).
  • Takao (JR or Keiō). The JR Chūō Line (Rapid) for Nakano, Tachikawa and Takao uses platforms 11 and 12. Keiō says its line runs from Shinjuku to Takaosanguchi, for Mount Takao; use the Central West Gate (Keiō exit).

We did not check times or fares for these trips beyond what is written here. Check the operator before you go.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time is the last Narita Express from Shinjuku?

JR East's timetable (edition 2612) shows the last Narita Express from Shinjuku at 19:39, on weekdays and on Saturdays and holidays. The first is at 5:55. The trains run to Narita Airport Terminal 1, via Terminal 2·3, and JR East's station map shows them on platforms 5 and 6. One sample train leaves Shinjuku at 10:08 and reaches Terminal 1 at 11:27, about 80 minutes. Timetables change, so check JR East before you travel.

What if I miss the last Narita Express?

The Airport Limousine Bus leaves from the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal (Busta Shinjuku), 4th floor, to Narita and Haneda. We did not verify the last buses, so check the bus company's live timetable before you rely on one.

Which exit do I use for Busta Shinjuku and the airport buses?

Use the JR New South Gate on the 2nd floor. Busta Shinjuku says the New South Gate is open from 5:45 to 24:00 and that outside those hours you should use the South Gate. Buses leave from the 4th floor and arrive on the 3rd floor.

Should I use the West exit or the East exit?

The West side is for the Odakyū and Keiō lines, the Toei Shinjuku and Ōedo lines, the West exit bus stop and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building, about 10 minutes on foot from the west exit (GO TOKYO). The East side is for Kabukichō and Seibu-Shinjuku: the Godzilla head on the Shinjuku Toho Building is about 5 minutes from the east exit. Busta Shinjuku and the JR EAST Travel Service Center are by the New South Gate on the 2nd floor. The B1 Free Passage links the East and West sides outside the ticket gates.

Which platform is the Yamanote Line at Shinjuku?

JR East's station map shows platform 14 for Harajuku, Shibuya and Shinagawa, and platform 15 for Ikebukuro, Tabata and Ueno.

Where can I leave my luggage at Shinjuku Station?

Busta Shinjuku has coin lockers on the 4th floor, for up to 72 hours. You need the QR code on the receipt to open them. The Tokyo Tourist Information Center on Busta's 3rd floor (6:30 to 21:00) offers temporary safekeeping and parcel delivery. The Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center keeps bags from 8:00 to 16:00, with pick-up until 19:00. JR East's map shows coin lockers in the station too, but we did not find their prices.

Where is the JR EAST Travel Service Center at Shinjuku?

JR East says it is on the right after you exit the New South Ticket Gate, on the 2nd floor. It is open 8:00 to 19:00 all year, sells JR East passes and tickets, hands out tickets booked on JR-EAST Train Reservation, exchanges currency (Travelex) and has free Wi-Fi. It does not list luggage storage.

Where is there an ATM at Shinjuku Station?

Busta Shinjuku has a Seven Bank ATM in its waiting room, open 3:30 to 25:00 (1 am). Seven Bank says its ATMs take cards issued outside Japan. Tokyo Metro's station map also marks ATMs on B1F and B2F. To change money, the JR EAST Travel Service Center has Travelex (8:00 to 19:00) and the Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center exchanges currency from 8:00 to 16:00.

How do I get to Hakone from Shinjuku?

Odakyū's Romancecar limited express runs direct from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto in about 80 minutes. All seats are reserved, and you need a limited express ticket plus a regular fare ticket. The Odakyū Sightseeing Service Center (B1, West Underground Exit, 8:00 to 16:00) sells Romancecar tickets and the Hakone Freepass.

Is there English help at Shinjuku Station?

JR East lists English information at the 2F South Gate (10:00 to 18:00 inside the gates, 10:00 to 17:00 outside) and inside the B1 West Gate (10:00 to 17:00). Busta Shinjuku's information counter is open 7:00 to 23:00 and says it supports English and Japanese at all hours.

Why are some Marunouchi Line exits closed?

Tokyo Metro's station map (as of November 2025) marks many Shinjuku exits as closed during construction, for example A11, A13, A14, B9 and B13. Follow the signs, and ask station staff if a route is closed.
