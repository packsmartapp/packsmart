Haneda on one page

  • Where: Haneda is in southern Tokyo, about 15 to 20 minutes by train from Shinagawa or Hamamatsucho.
  • Terminals: there are 3. Most international flights use Terminal 3. A free shuttle bus links all three terminals.
  • Before you fly: register on Visit Japan Web (free) and keep the QR codes on your phone. Every traveller, children included, needs their own QR code.
  • After landing: immigration, then baggage, then customs, then the arrival lobby on the 2nd floor.
  • In the arrival lobby: the Tourist Information Center (open 24 hours), SIM and Wi-Fi counters, cash machines, and the gates for the Keikyu Line and Tokyo Monorail.
  • Free Wi-Fi: connect to HANEDA-FREE-WIFI for quick messages.
  • Need help? Every terminal has information counters, or call +81 3-5757-8111 (5 am to midnight).

Which terminal?

Haneda has three terminals. Check your ticket or airline app, because airlines can change terminals.

TerminalWho uses itTrain station
Terminal 3Most international flights.Its own stations: Keikyu "Haneda Airport Terminal 3" and Tokyo Monorail "Terminal 3". The ticket gates are on the 2nd-floor arrival lobby.
Terminal 1Domestic flights, mainly Japan Airlines (JAL).Keikyu "Terminal 1·2" and Tokyo Monorail "Terminal 1", on the basement floor.
Terminal 2Domestic flights, mainly ANA. It also has an international area used by some airlines.Keikyu "Terminal 1·2" and Tokyo Monorail "Terminal 2", on the basement floor.
Moving between terminals: a free shuttle bus links all three. Boarding is on the 1st floor: Terminal 3 stop 0, Terminal 1 stop 8, Terminal 2 stop 9. Haneda says buses run every 4 minutes from 5 am to 8 pm and every 8 minutes until midnight (first and last buses differ a little by route). The underground walkway between Terminals 1 and 2 was out of service for construction when we checked on 18 November 2026. See Haneda's page on travelling between terminals.

Your first hour after landing

Most people follow the same steps. Signs are in Japanese and English, and staff are nearby if you are unsure. These are the steps Haneda lists for international arrivals (the same at Terminals 2 and 3).

  1. Before you fly (optional but helpful). Register on the free Visit Japan Web site. You enter your immigration and customs details once and get QR codes. Take a screenshot of them in case your signal is weak.
  2. Quarantine. Haneda lists this as the first step. If your flight handed out a health form, fill it in. If you became unwell on the way, tell an officer.
  3. Immigration. Have your passport ready, and your arrival card if you filled one in. Haneda has combined immigration and customs kiosks at Terminals 2 and 3 (since April 2025): scan your Visit Japan Web QR code there. The automatic gates do not stamp your passport: ask the staff after the gate if you would like a stamp.
  4. Baggage claim. Find your flight on the screens and collect your bags. If a bag is missing or damaged, go to your airline staff with your baggage tag.
  5. Customs. Show your Visit Japan Web QR code, or a paper declaration form (many flights hand these out). If you carry meat, fruit, plants or animals, Haneda lists an animal and plant quarantine step before customs.

Then you arrive in the arrival lobby on the 2nd floor. Here you find the Tourist Information Center (open 24 hours), SIM and pocket Wi-Fi counters, currency exchange, cash machines, and the gates for the Keikyu Line and Tokyo Monorail. Bus tickets are sold from ticket machines and a bus counter, and taxis leave from the 1st floor (stop 21).

Rules change. Immigration and customs procedures are updated often. If anything here differs from what the signs or officers tell you, follow the signs and officers.

Sources: Haneda Airport: international arrival procedures, Tourist Information Center and taxi stands. Checked 18 November 2026.

A train card (Suica or PASMO)

A tap-on travel card saves you buying a ticket for every train, subway or bus ride, and it also pays in many shops and vending machines. Visitors usually buy a tourist version, called Welcome Suica. It has no deposit, it is valid for 28 days, and any money left on it cannot be refunded.

  • Where at Haneda: the JR East Travel Service Center and the dedicated Welcome Suica ticket machines at Haneda Airport Terminal 3 (Tokyo Monorail) Station. In principle only one card is sold per person. Sales points change, so check JR East's Welcome Suica page before you fly. The Tourist Information Center in the Terminal 3 arrival lobby (open 24 hours) handles JR tickets and can point you the right way.
  • Which trains: IC cards such as Suica and PASMO are used on many Tokyo trains, subways and buses. Look for the card symbol on the ticket gate.
  • iPhone users: an app version, Welcome Suica Mobile, lets you set up before you land (iPhone only, as of April 2026).
  • Staying longer than 28 days? A regular Suica or PASMO may suit you better. Ask at a ticket counter.
This changes often. Sales points and card types have changed several times in recent years. Check JR East's Welcome Suica page before you travel.

Sending your suitcase to your hotel

If you do not want to carry heavy bags on a crowded train, you can post them ahead. Haneda's floor maps list delivery counters run by Yamato, JAL and Sagawa. Ask which counter is in your terminal.

  • Terminal 3. Delivery counters (Yamato and JAL) are on the 2nd floor, in the arrival area, and on the 3rd floor, in the departure area.
  • Usually next day, with one same-day option. Most airport delivery arrives the next day. JAL runs a same-day service for any airline's passengers from the JAL ABC counter in Terminal 3 (4 am to 6 pm) to hotels in central Tokyo, at ¥4,500 per suitcase. Bags handed in before 4 pm are due at the hotel by 8 pm, and bags handed in before 6 pm by 10 pm. JAL says delivery can be late, or not possible, in bad weather or heavy traffic. JAL same-day delivery page.
  • Prices vary by size and company. JAL ABC lists ¥1,950 for a bag up to 120 cm (length, width and height added together) sent to Tokyo, Kanagawa, Chiba or Saitama. Ask for the price at the counter before you hand your bag over.
  • Have these ready: your hotel name, address and phone number, written down.
  • Keep with you: medicines, valuables, chargers and a change of clothes.

Prefer to book it online? You can book a luggage delivery service between Tokyo hotels and the airport (Haneda or Narita), a partner link. Check the listing for sizes, prices and cut-off times.

Nursing rooms

Nursing rooms are marked on the floor maps of every terminal. The maps show the symbol on several floors but do not name each room, so ask at an information counter for the nearest one.

If you need help

Every terminal has information counters. If you are tired, unsure or carrying a lot, go straight to a counter instead of searching on your own.

Help for older travellers and anyone who needs assistance

You do not have to cope alone. Haneda is set up to help, and asking early is easier than struggling.

  • Information counters. Every terminal has them, and Haneda's floor maps show where. Staff can answer questions and solve problems.
  • Phone help. Haneda's general information line is +81 3-5757-8111, open every day from 5 am to midnight.
  • Special assistance. Haneda's service guide lists wheelchair and stroller rental and special-assistance services. To arrange help from the aircraft door to baggage claim or the station, contact your airline.
  • Trains and buses. Tell station or bus staff if you need help. Haneda's website also lists accessibility information for trains and buses.
  • Before you fly. Ask your airline to arrange assistance at the airport when you book.

Sources: Haneda Airport's floor maps for Terminal 1, Terminal 2 and Terminal 3 (PDF), and its Tourist Information Center page. Checked 18 November 2026.

Leaving Haneda

  • Know your terminal. Most international flights leave from Terminal 3, which has its own Keikyu and Monorail stations. Some airlines use Terminal 2's international area. Check your ticket before you set off.
  • Return rented gear first. If you rented a pocket Wi-Fi, check how to return it. Some companies take it back at the airport counter, others at a postbox.
  • Allow time. Check-in, security and passport control all take time, so arrive early. Your airline sets its own advice for international flights, so check your ticket.
  • Shopped tax-free? From 1 November 2026 you pay the tax at the shop and claim it back at the airport. Within 90 days of buying, scan your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal before you board, with your receipts and the items with you, because they may be inspected and cannot be checked in first. Haneda lists the terminals on its tax refund page: Terminal 1 has no tax refund service, so use Terminal 2 or 3, where the procedure terminals are before the 3rd-floor security check. Terminal 3's are open 24 hours. Allow extra time, as queues are likely while the system is new (checked 18 November 2026).

A short stop or a long layover

If you have less than about 4 hours between flights, stay in the airport. Immigration, the train ride and the trip back eat up the time. Haneda is close to central Tokyo, so with a longer wait a short trip is realistic: leaving the airport means entering Japan, so you need the visa or visa exemption that applies to your passport (check with your airline or the Japanese embassy), and you should leave plenty of time to get back. You can also rest inside the airport: The Royal Park Hotel Tokyo Haneda has transit rooms on the airside of Terminal 3, and the HANEDA AIRPORT GARDEN complex next to Terminal 3 has a tourist-information centre with luggage storage and currency exchange.

Landing late or leaving very early?

Haneda has a hotel in each terminal, according to the airport's own service page: First Cabin Haneda Terminal 1 (a capsule hotel on the 1st-floor arrival lobby), Haneda Excel Hotel Tokyu in Terminal 2 (2nd-floor terminal lobby, north), and The Royal Park Hotel Tokyo Haneda in Terminal 3, with its entrance in the 3rd-floor departure lobby and short-stay transit rooms on the airside. Check each hotel's own page for check-in times and whether it has space.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Haneda Airport in Tokyo?

Yes. Haneda is in Ota Ward, in the south of Tokyo, much closer to the centre than Narita. Haneda's own website gives about 15 minutes by train to Shinagawa, 20 to Hamamatsucho, 30 to Yokohama and 40 to Asakusa.

Which terminal will my flight use?

Most international flights use Terminal 3. Terminal 2 also has an international area that some airlines use. Terminals 1 and 2 are mainly for domestic flights (Terminal 1 mainly Japan Airlines, Terminal 2 mainly ANA). Check your ticket or airline app, because airlines can change terminals.

How do I get between the terminals?

A free shuttle bus links all three. Boarding is on the 1st floor: Terminal 3 stop 0, Terminal 1 stop 8, Terminal 2 stop 9. Haneda says buses run every 4 minutes from 5 am to 8 pm and every 8 minutes until midnight. The underground walkway between Terminals 1 and 2 was out of service for construction when we checked.

Is there free Wi-Fi at Haneda?

Yes. Look for the network named HANEDA-FREE-WIFI. It works for quick messages, but it is not meant for a full day of maps and apps.

Do I need Visit Japan Web?

It is not required, because you can still use paper forms. But registering on the free Visit Japan Web site before you fly lets you show QR codes for immigration and customs, which can speed up your arrival.

How do I get from Haneda to central Tokyo?

By Keikyu Line train (to Shinagawa, Yokohama and Asakusa), by Tokyo Monorail (to Hamamatsucho, then JR), by Airport Limousine bus, by taxi, or by a pre-booked private transfer. Fares and times change, so check the operator before you go.

Is there a prayer room at Haneda Airport?

Yes, in Terminals 2 and 3. Haneda's service page lists two rooms on the 3rd floor of Terminal 3 (before security), and in Terminal 2's international area two on the 3rd floor (before security) and two on the 2nd floor (after security). There is none in Terminal 1. Opening hours are not listed, so ask at an information counter.

Where can I find a nursing room or a quiet space?

Nursing rooms are marked on the floor maps of every terminal. Haneda also has small "Calm down, cool down" spaces for people who find crowds or noise very hard. Ask at an information counter for the nearest one.

What if I land late at night?

Trains run from about 5 am until around midnight, so a very late arrival may miss the last one. Late-night buses and taxis run after that, and there are hotels inside the terminals. Check the last train for your route before you book.

Can I connect to a domestic flight at Haneda?

Yes. Terminal 3 has a transfer area on the 2nd floor for passengers changing between international and domestic flights. Rules for bags and customs depend on your airline and tickets, so ask your airline before you fly.
