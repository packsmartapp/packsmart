Osaka is Japan's big food city and the easiest base for the wider Kansai region. This guide starts with the experiences that are hardest to organise on your own: a private car with a driver, Kobe beef and sake tours, and a sumo show with wagyu sukiyaki. It then covers the day trips people make most often, and the main sights in the city. Facts come from official tourism and attraction sites, checked on 19 November 2026. We print no prices, because they change by date and group size.

Osaka on one page

  • Two centres: Umeda (also called Kita), around Osaka Station in the north, and Namba and Dotonbori (Minami) in the south. The Midosuji subway line links them in about 15 minutes.
  • Bay area: Universal Studios Japan (USJ) and the Kaiyukan aquarium. USJ is about 10 to 11 minutes by JR train from Osaka Station. Kaiyukan is a 5-minute walk from Osakako Station.
  • Easy day trips: Kobe about 20 to 25 minutes by train. Himeji about 30 minutes by Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka. Hiroshima about 1 hour 25 minutes by Shinkansen. Koyasan at least 90 minutes from Namba, plus a 5-minute cable car.
  • Hard to arrange alone: a private car with a driver, a Kobe beef and sake tour, and a sumo show with wagyu sukiyaki. They come first in this guide.
  • Food: takoyaki, okonomiyaki and kushikatsu are the three Osaka classics.
  • Arriving? See our Kansai Airport guide if you fly in, or our Osaka station guide if you come by bullet train.

Kobe beef and sake: private tours

Kobe is about 20 to 25 minutes from Osaka by train, and two famous things meet there: Kobe beef and the Nada sake district. Several operators run private tours from Osaka that combine them, with hotel pick-up, so you do not need to arrange trains and restaurant tables yourself. The tours below are for private or small groups. Some are newly listed and have few reviews, so read the reviews, the meeting point and the cancellation terms before you book.

What counts as Kobe beef

The Kobe Beef Marketing and Distribution Promotion Association defines Kobe beef as beef from Tajima cattle raised in Hyogo Prefecture, and the name is registered as a geographical indication in Japan. Its FAQ says Kobe beef is less than about 0.2 percent of the beef eaten in Japan. When you book a beef lunch or dinner, ask whether the beef is certified Kobe beef, and which cut and portion you are getting. The association explains the rules on its English FAQ page.

Nada sake: the do-it-yourself version

Nada-Gogo, the "five villages of Nada" between Kobe and Nishinomiya, is Japan's biggest sake-producing area, according to VISIT KOBE. Displays and shops at the larger breweries are free to visit, but special tours and some facilities need advance booking. From Umeda it takes about 20 minutes on the direct Hanshin line, and VISIT KOBE publishes a walking route of about five hours if you want to plan it yourself.

Sumo show with wagyu sukiyaki

A sumo show is a live demonstration by former professional wrestlers, with an explanation of the rules and rituals, usually a chance for guests to step into the ring, and an optional meal. The two listings below offer wagyu sukiyaki, and one also offers chanko nabe, the traditional sumo hot pot. The Konishiki listing says that former Ozeki Konishiki supervises the show but does not appear in the regular performances. The Konishiki listing offers child rates, and children aged 6 and under enter free.

Sumo shows with a meal

Front-row seats with wagyu sukiyaki, or the longer Konishiki-supervised show.

Where it is: the listings we saw on 18 and 19 November place these shows in Tajiri or Izumisano, in the south of Osaka Prefecture near Kansai Airport, not in central Osaka. Check the meeting point and the travel time before you book. The front-row listing runs about 2.5 hours, and the Konishiki listing 1.5 to 4 hours depending on the options you add.

Want the real tournament?

The Grand Sumo tournament in Osaka is held every March at EDION Arena Osaka, a five-minute walk from Nankai Namba Station. The Japan Sumo Association lists the 2027 March tournament as 14 to 28 March 2027, with advance tickets on sale from 6 February 2027. Tickets are sold through the association's ticket pages. That is a different event from the shows above.

Himeji Castle, with Arima Onsen and Mount Rokko

Osaka Info calls Himeji Castle arguably the best-preserved castle in Japan, and UNESCO listed it in 1993. The castle is open daily 9:00 to 17:00, with the last entry at 16:00, and it closes on 29 and 30 December. From Himeji Station it is a 15 to 20 minute walk, or about 5 minutes by bus. The city says a visit usually takes 90 to 120 minutes. The castle's official pages recommend digital time-slot tickets, and the city's page says same-day tickets may not be on sale at some times of day. Read the official Himeji Castle page for the day you go.

Koyasan and Okunoin

Koyasan is the birthplace of Shingon Buddhism, brought to Japan by the monk Kukai (also known as Kobo Daishi), and Okunoin Temple is his mausoleum. JNTO gives the route from Osaka as the Nankai Koya Line from Namba to Gokurakubashi, taking at least 90 minutes, then a 5-minute cable car up the mountain. It is a long day by train, so a guided small-group tour that handles the transfers is a common choice.

Hiroshima and Miyajima

From Shin-Osaka the Shinkansen reaches Hiroshima in about 1 hour 25 minutes, according to JNTO. Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park remembers the 1945 atomic bombing, and Itsukushima Shrine on Miyajima Island is known for its torii gate standing in the sea. From Hiroshima Station a local train to Miyajimaguchi takes about 25 to 30 minutes, then the ferry takes about 10 minutes.

The main sights in the city

Osaka Castle

Toyotomi Hideyoshi began the castle in 1583. The main tower you see today was rebuilt in 1931 and has 8 floors inside, with a view of the city from the top. Osaka Info gives a walk of just under 20 minutes to the keep from JR Osakajokoen Station, over Gokuraku-bashi Bridge, or from Morinomiya Station. Its pages list the museum hours as 9:00 to 17:00 or 18:00 depending on the season, with the last entry 30 minutes before closing, and the castle is closed from 28 December to 1 January. Check the castle's own site for your date.

Dotonbori and Namba

Dotonbori is Osaka's best-known dining and entertainment district, with neon signs along the canal, the Glico sign and the giant crab above the Kani Doraku restaurant. JNTO suggests walking it at night. It is in the Minami area, close to Namba.

Umeda Sky Building

The Floating Garden Observatory is an open-air rooftop 173 metres above the ground, reached by a see-through escalator from the 35th to the 39th floor. Osaka Info lists it as open 9:30 to 22:30, last entry 22:00. Hours can be shorter on event days. If you buy an Osaka e-Pass, admission is free until 3 pm.

Shinsekai and Tsutenkaku

Tsutenkaku Tower's main observation deck is open every day from 9:00 to 21:45, with the last entry at 21:15, according to Osaka Info. The Shinsekai streets below are where kushikatsu began.

Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan

Kaiyukan shows about 30,000 animals of around 620 species, and its whale shark is the best known. It opens 10:00 to 20:00 with the last entry an hour before closing, hours vary by season, and it closes for two days in January. The aquarium recommends booking a timed e-ticket, and Osaka Info lists about two hours for a visit.

Universal Studios Japan

USJ is about 10 to 11 minutes by JR train from Osaka Station to Universal City Station, with no change. Opening hours change from day to day, so check the park's official calendar for the day you plan to go.

What to eat

Osakans are known for the saying kuidaore, which JNTO translates as "eat until you fall over". Three dishes come first:

  • Takoyaki: octopus dumplings cooked in a round pan and sold at street stalls. Osaka Info says shops began to appear in the city in the 1950s.
  • Okonomiyaki: a savoury pancake of batter and cabbage with pork, seafood or both. The name means "grill what you like".
  • Kushikatsu: skewered meat and vegetables, battered and deep-fried. Many restaurants have a "no double dipping" rule for the shared sauce. Kushikatsu started in Shinsekai.

Dotonbori and Shinsekai are the two best places to try all three on one evening.

Getting to and around Osaka

  • By plane: most international flights use Kansai Airport. Our Kansai Airport guide covers the terminals, SIM options and the trains and buses into the city.
  • By bullet train: you arrive at Shin-Osaka, not at Osaka Station. Our Osaka station guide explains the difference and how to get between them.
  • Across the city: JR lines, Osaka Metro and the private railways all connect. The Midosuji subway line runs from Umeda to Namba in about 15 minutes. ICOCA works on JR trains in Kansai.
  • Passes: Osaka Info runs the Osaka e-Pass and the Osaka Amazing Pass. Each lists the sights it covers, so compare it with your plan before buying.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far are Kobe, Himeji, Hiroshima and Koyasan from Osaka?

Kobe is about 20 to 25 minutes by JR train to Sannomiya. Himeji is about 30 minutes by Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka, or about 1 hour by JR Special Rapid from Osaka. Hiroshima is about 1 hour 25 minutes by Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka, according to JNTO, and Miyajima needs a local train and a ferry on top. Koyasan is at least 90 minutes from Namba on the Nankai Koya Line, then a 5-minute cable car.

What does a private car with driver in Osaka include?

On the two booking listings we checked on 18 November 2026, the driver and vehicle are included, and admission to sights is not. One is a 10-hour charter with free pick-up and drop-off inside Osaka city, and the other runs 8 to 18 hours with hotel pick-up. Drivers speak Mandarin or Japanese on the first, and a language (Chinese, Japanese or English) assigned at random on the second, with a translation device or software for English, so confirm the language before you book.

What is the difference between a sumo show and the Grand Sumo tournament?

A sumo show is a demonstration by former professional wrestlers, with an explanation of the rules, a chance to try the ring and an optional meal. The Grand Sumo tournament is the official competition. In Osaka it is held every March at EDION Arena Osaka, and the Japan Sumo Association lists the 2027 tournament as 14 to 28 March 2027.

What is Kobe beef?

The Kobe Beef Marketing and Distribution Promotion Association defines it as beef from Tajima cattle raised in Hyogo Prefecture. It is registered as a geographical indication in Japan and is less than about 0.2 percent of the beef eaten there. Ask a restaurant whether its beef is certified Kobe beef.

How do I get to USJ and Kaiyukan?

USJ is about 10 to 11 minutes by JR train from Osaka Station to Universal City Station, with no change. Kaiyukan is a 5-minute walk from Osakako Station on the Osaka Metro Chuo Line.

When is the Osaka sumo tournament?

Every March at EDION Arena Osaka. The Japan Sumo Association lists the 2027 March tournament as 14 to 28 March 2027, with advance tickets on sale from 6 February 2027.
