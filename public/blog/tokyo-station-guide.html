Tokyo Station on one page

  • Two sides: the Marunouchi side (west) has the red-brick building, the Narita Express route and the JR East Travel Service Center. The Yaesu side (east) is the one Tokyo Station City recommends for bullet trains, and has the airport buses and First Avenue.
  • Between the sides: the central passage on the 1st floor. Allow about 10 minutes (Tokyo Station City).
  • Shinkansen platforms: 14 to 19 for the Tokaido and Sanyo lines (Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima), 20 to 23 for the Tohoku, Joetsu and Hokuriku lines. Check the board inside the blue ticket gates.
  • Narita Express: from the Marunouchi Central Gate, down to B1, B4, then B5. Usually platform 3 or 4. All seats reserved, about an hour to Narita. Check the board.
  • Local trains: several lines share platform numbers 1 to 4. Look for the line name as well as the number.
  • Metro: the Marunouchi Line has its own station, M17.
  • Luggage: coin lockers around the station, and day storage at the JR East Travel Service Center (left after the Marunouchi North ticket gate).
  • Need help? Ask station staff or a JR East Information Center. In an emergency, call 119.

Which side: Marunouchi or Yaesu?

Tokyo Station is one huge building with two main sides, joined by a central passage on the 1st floor. The Marunouchi side is to the west, and it is the one with the red-brick station building. The Yaesu side is to the east. Tokyo Station City tells people using Smart EX bullet train tickets to use the Yaesu side, and says the Marunouchi side is about a 10-minute walk from it.

Both sides have a North, a Central and a South gate, so a name like “North Exit” means two different places. Say which side as well, and follow the signs for the place you want.

Tokyo Station City lists two groups of Shinkansen platforms. Your ticket and the departure boards show your platform, and platforms can change, so check before you walk up.

  • Finding the platform. Tokyo Station City's route: on the 1st floor, head toward the central information counter along the central passage, look for the blue ticket gates, then check the platform on the electronic board inside the gate. Wait at your car number.
  • Which side. Tokyo Station City recommends the Yaesu side for Smart EX tickets and says the Marunouchi side is about a 10-minute walk away.
  • Booking. JR East's reservation service cannot book the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen, so use JR Central's Smart EX or a ticket office for Kyoto and Osaka. JR East says the JR TOKYO Wide Pass is not valid on the Tokaido Shinkansen.
  • Reserved or not. Non-reserved tickets are valid on the day of purchase, but JR East says trains such as the Hayabusa and Hayate are all reserved. Check your train.
  • Big suitcases. Over 160 cm (sum of three sides) up to 250 cm needs a seat with an oversized baggage area on the Tokaido and Sanyo lines. See the luggage section for the full table.
  • Tokyo to Kyoto. About 130 minutes on the fastest trains (JR Central).

Narita Express and the airports

The Narita Express (N’EX) uses underground platforms. Tokyo Station City gives this route: from the Marunouchi Central Gate, go down to B1, then to B4, then to B5, and the N’EX leaves from platforms 3 and 4.

  • Check the board. JR East's timetables also show some N’EX trains on tracks 1 and 2, so treat 3 or 4 as the usual platforms and confirm on the departure board.
  • Board the right train. Tokyo Station City says the Narita-bound platform also serves a regular rapid train, so make sure you board the N’EX.
  • Reserved seats. All N’EX seats are reserved. JR East's timetables give roughly an hour between Narita Airport and Tokyo Station.
  • Airport Limousine Bus. The bus company lists a stop at the Yaesu North Exit (Tekko Building) for both Haneda and Narita. We did not find a fare we could confirm, so we give none.
  • Haneda by train. Take the JR Yamanote Line to Hamamatsucho, then the Tokyo Monorail. Tokyo Monorail says the express takes 13 minutes from Hamamatsucho to Terminal 3.

More detail: our Narita Airport guide and Haneda Airport guide.

Local trains, Metro and Disney

JNTO and JR East list these lines at Tokyo Station: the Yamanote Line, the Chuo Rapid Line, the Keihin-Tohoku Line, the Sobu Rapid Line, the Yokosuka Line, the Keiyo and Musashino Lines and the Narita Express. Tokyo Station City says platforms 1 to 4 are shared by several of them, so check the line name as well as the number.

  • Yamanote Line. The loop line. It links Tokyo Station with Hamamatsucho, where you change for the monorail to Haneda.
  • Tokyo Metro. The Marunouchi Line has its own station, M17. JNTO says long underground passages link Tokyo Station with Otemachi Station, where the Tozai, Chiyoda, Hanzomon and Toei Mita lines stop. It is a bit of a walk.
  • Tokyo Disney Resort. Take the Keiyo or Musashino Line to Maihama Station, about 15 minutes according to Tokyo Disney Resort. Tokyo Disneyland is a short walk from the station. For Tokyo DisneySea, change at Resort Gateway Station to the Disney Resort Line. We did not find an official walking time from the Keiyo platform, so allow extra time.
  • Mount Fuji and Kawaguchiko. JNTO's tourist information schedules list buses from Tokyo Station. We did not confirm times or fares.

Sources: Tokyo Station City, JNTO, Tokyo Disney Resort and JR East. Checked 19 November 2026. Times change, so check the operator.

Money and ATMs

Seven Bank's ATM finder lists the hours below and says hours may differ. Seven Bank ATMs are also in 7-Eleven shops, but the shop ATMs here are not open 24 hours.

  • Seven Bank ATMs, Yaesu side. Outside the ticket gates: at the Yaesu Central and South gates, 5:00 to 24:00; in First Avenue Tokyo Station, 1F and B1 (two of them in Tokyo Ramen Street), 6:00 to 24:00; in GRANROOF, B1, 4:00 to 24:30. Tokyo Station City also lists one at the 1F Nihombashi Gate.
  • Seven Bank ATMs inside the Shinkansen gates. Inside the Shinkansen Central and South gates, 5:30 to 23:50.
  • Marunouchi side. Tokyo Station City lists a Seven Bank ATM outside the 1F Marunouchi North Gate. We did not find its hours. The Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line station (M17) has two Seven Bank ATMs, 5:00 to 24:00: one on B1 outside the Metro gates toward exit 4a, and one near the Metro station’s north ticket gate.
  • More Seven Bank ATMs. GRANSTA Yaekita (Seven Bank says “inside Tokyo Station”), 7:00 to 24:00, and GRANROOF FRONT, 6:00 to 23:00.
  • Other ATMs. Tokyo Station City also lists Japan Post Bank ATMs (1F Yaesu South Gate, 1F Nihombashi Gate, B1 GRANROOF), JR East’s VIEW ALTTE machines, and Mizuho, MUFG, Resona and SMBC ATMs. It does not say which take cards issued outside Japan, and we did not check them. JNTO lists Japan Post Bank and Seven Bank ATMs as ones that take international cards.
  • Cards from abroad. Seven Bank says its ATMs accept cards issued outside Japan with the Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, American Express, JCB, Discover and Diners Club marks, but some cards with these marks may not be accepted, and fees vary by card brand. You choose the language, including English, after you insert the card. Visa and Mastercard users are asked whether to be charged in their home currency or in yen. Visa and Mastercard work round the clock; the other brands stop for a few minutes around midnight. With a card from abroad you can take out up to 100,000 yen at a time (30,000 yen for magnetic-stripe transactions).
  • Currency exchange. The JR EAST Travel Service Center, outside the 1F Marunouchi North Gate. Tokyo Station City lists 11:00 to 19:00 and 31 currencies, but JR East and JNTO print other hours, so check JR East’s page before you go. GO TOKYO’s Marunouchi map also shows a foreign currency exchange machine on 1F of the Marunouchi Building, about 1 minute from the station.

Paying for trains: IC cards

An IC card lets you tap in and out of most trains and buses in Japan without buying a ticket each time.

  • Welcome Suica. JR East says it lasts 28 days and needs no deposit. In principle one card is sold to each person. You load 1,000 to 10,000 yen when you buy it, and can top up with cash to 20,000 yen. JR East says it works on trains, buses and in shops across Japan, but not in stores in the PiTaPa area.
  • Where to buy. The JR East Travel Service Center at Tokyo Station sells it (left after the Marunouchi North ticket gate). It is also sold at JAPAN RAIL CAFE TOKYO on the Yaesu side, and by machines at Narita and Haneda airports.
  • Lockers. JR East says a Welcome Suica card can pay for a coin locker and unlock it.
  • Tourist PASMO. JNTO also lists the TOURIST PASMO as a deposit-free card for visitors.
  • Phone version. JR East also has Welcome Suica Mobile. Check JR East's page for which phones it supports.

Sources: JR East and JNTO. Checked 19 November 2026. Rules and prices change, so check before you buy.

Lockers and luggage

Tokyo Station City describes the station as 28 platforms and about 182,000 square metres, so it is worth sorting out your bags before you start walking. You have three choices: a coin locker, the storage counter, or sending the bag to your hotel.

  • Coin lockers. Tokyo Station City links to a locker search and reservation tool on its website. JNTO warns that lockers at very busy stations fill up quickly. A Welcome Suica card can pay for a locker and unlock it, according to JR East. We did not find an official list of sizes and fees, so we give none.
  • JR East Travel Service Center. JR East says it keeps luggage for the day only. It is on your left after the Marunouchi North ticket gate on the 1st floor. Opening hours differ between JR East and JNTO, so check JR East's page before you go.
  • Sending a bag to your hotel. JNTO's hands-free travel page, an older page, names a delivery counter at the Marunouchi North Exit. We did not confirm that it is current, or its cut-off time, so check JR East's page for the delivery counter.
  • Keep with you: medicines, valuables, chargers and a change of clothes.
  • Have these ready: your hotel name, address and phone number, written down.

Help for older travellers and anyone who needs assistance

You do not have to cope alone. Asking early is easier than struggling.

  • Information centres. JR East Information Centers (10 am to 6 pm) are at the 1st-floor central passage, the 1st-floor Marunouchi Central Exit and the B1 Marunouchi Underground Central Exit, inside the ticket gates. The JR East Travel Service Center is on your left after the Marunouchi North ticket gate.
  • Station staff. Tell staff at a ticket gate or ticket office if you need help or are not sure which way to go.
  • Allow extra time. The station is very large, and the Marunouchi and Yaesu sides are about a 10-minute walk apart, according to Tokyo Station City. We did not find official walking times between gates and platforms, so add a buffer, especially with luggage.
  • Lifts and toilets. We did not find an official list of lift and toilet locations in the pages we read. Follow the signs, or ask staff.
  • Send your suitcase ahead. See the luggage section above.
  • Visitor hotline. The Japan Visitor Hotline, 050-3816-2787, is open 24 hours and helps in English, Chinese and Korean. In an emergency, call 119.

Sources: Tokyo Station City, JR East and JNTO, checked 19 November 2026.

Leaving Tokyo by train or bus

  • Know your side. Bullet trains, the airport limousine buses and highway buses are on the Yaesu side. The Narita Express route starts on the Marunouchi side.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which exit should I use at Tokyo Station: Marunouchi or Yaesu?

Tokyo Station has two main sides. The Marunouchi side is the west side, with the red-brick station building, and the Yaesu side is the east side. For bullet train tickets bought on Smart EX, Tokyo Station City recommends the Yaesu side and describes the Marunouchi side as about a 10-minute walk away. The route to the Narita Express platform starts at the Marunouchi Central Gate, and the JR East Travel Service Center is on the Marunouchi side. The airport limousine buses leave from the Yaesu North Exit.

Which platforms are the Shinkansen at Tokyo Station?

Tokyo Station City lists platforms 14 to 19 for the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen (to Nagoya, Kyoto, Shin-Osaka, Hiroshima and Hakata) and platforms 20 to 23 for the Tohoku, Joetsu and Hokuriku Shinkansen (to Sendai, Nagano, Echigo-Yuzawa and Kanazawa). On the 1st floor, head toward the central information counter along the central passage, look for the blue ticket gates, and check the platform on the electronic board inside the gate.

Where is the Narita Express platform?

Tokyo Station City says the Narita Express leaves from underground platforms 3 and 4. From the Marunouchi Central Gate, go down to B1, then to B4, then to the B5 platform level. JR East's timetables also show some trains using tracks 1 and 2, so check the departure board. Several platforms in the station share the numbers 1 to 4, so look for the line name as well as the number. The Narita Express is all reserved seating, and JR East's timetables give roughly an hour from Narita Airport.

Can I use the JR Tokyo Wide Pass on the Tokaido Shinkansen?

No. JR East says the JR TOKYO Wide Pass cannot be used on the Tokaido Shinkansen, because that line is run by a different company, JR Central. JR East's reservation service also cannot book the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen, so use JR Central's Smart EX or a ticket office.

How big a suitcase can I take on the Shinkansen?

On the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen, JR Central says a bag with three sides adding up to more than 160 cm, up to 250 cm, needs a seat with an oversized baggage area, reserved in advance. Bags of 160 cm or less go on the overhead racks, at your feet, or in the deck storage area with no extra booking. JR East says its Tohoku, Joetsu and Hokuriku lines have no designated seats for oversized luggage.

Where can I leave my luggage at Tokyo Station?

JR East's Travel Service Center offers temporary luggage storage, and items are kept for the day only. There are also coin lockers around the station, and Tokyo Station City links to a locker search and reservation tool. JNTO warns that lockers in very busy places fill up quickly. A Welcome Suica card can pay for a locker and unlock it. We did not find official locker prices, so we give none.

Where is the JR East Travel Service Center?

JR East says that after you pass through the Marunouchi North Exit ticket gate on the 1st floor, the center is on your left. It exchanges rail passes, sells passes and tickets, exchanges currency, stores luggage for the day, and has a prayer room open 10:00 to 17:00. There is also a counter inside JAPAN RAIL CAFE TOKYO on the Yaesu side, outside the Yaesu Central Exit. JR East and JNTO print different opening hours, so check JR East's page before you go.

Where can I buy a Welcome Suica?

JR East sells the Welcome Suica at its Travel Service Centers, including the one at Tokyo Station, at JAPAN RAIL CAFE, and from machines at Narita and Haneda airports. It lasts 28 days, needs no deposit and, in principle, only one is sold to each person. It works on trains, buses and in many shops across Japan. JNTO also lists the TOURIST PASMO as a deposit-free card for visitors.

How do I get from Tokyo Station to Tokyo Disney Resort?

Take the JR Keiyo Line (or the Musashino Line) to Maihama Station. Tokyo Disney Resort says it takes about 15 minutes, and Tokyo Disneyland is a short walk from the station. For Tokyo DisneySea, change at Resort Gateway Station to the Disney Resort Line monorail. The Keiyo platforms are a separate part of Tokyo Station, so follow the signs and give yourself extra time. We did not find an official walking time.

How do I get from Tokyo Station to Haneda Airport?

Tokyo Monorail says its Haneda Express takes 13 minutes from Hamamatsucho to Haneda Airport Terminal 3, and Hamamatsucho is on the JR Yamanote Line. The Airport Limousine Bus also runs from the Yaesu North Exit (Tekko Building) to Haneda, according to the bus company. Our Haneda guide has the full list of options.

Where can I eat in the station?

First Avenue Tokyo Station, on the Yaesu side underground, has Tokyo Ramen Street (10:30 to 23:00, last orders 22:30) and Tokyo Character Street (10:00 to 20:30), according to the First Avenue site. Inside the ticket gates, GRANSTA Tokyo has shops such as Ekibenya Matsuri for boxed meals (5:30 to 22:00). Hours differ by shop, so check before you go.

Is there a hotel inside Tokyo Station?

Yes. The Tokyo Station Hotel is inside the red-brick Marunouchi building, with its main entrance on the ground floor, reached directly from the JR Marunouchi South Gate, according to the hotel's access map.

How do I get to the Marunouchi Line and other Metro lines?

The Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line has its own Tokyo Station, number M17. JNTO adds that long underground passages link Tokyo Station with Otemachi Station, where the Tozai, Chiyoda, Hanzomon and Toei Mita lines stop, though it is a bit of a walk.
