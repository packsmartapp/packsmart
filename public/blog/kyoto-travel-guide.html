Kyoto is the easiest city in Japan to love and one of the hardest to plan, because the sights are spread over a wide area and many close at 17:00. This guide starts with the options that save the most effort: a private guide and car for the day, and a tea ceremony in a townhouse. It then covers the day trips people ask about most (Nara, and Amanohashidate with Ine Funaya), the main sights with their opening times, manners, food and getting around. Facts come from official tourism, temple and castle sites, checked on 19 November 2026. We print no prices, because they change by date and group size.

Kyoto on one page

  • Spread out: Kyoto Station is in the south. Higashiyama and Gion (Kiyomizu-dera) are in the east, Fushimi Inari in the south-east, Arashiyama in the west, and Kinkaku-ji in the north. Plan one or two areas a day.
  • Quick trains: Fushimi Inari is 5 minutes from Kyoto Station on the JR Nara Line. Saga-Arashiyama is about 11 minutes by Rapid on the JR Sagano Line. Nara is about 35 to 45 minutes.
  • Long day trip: Amanohashidate is about 2 to 2.5 hours each way, and Ine is about another hour by bus. Most visitors book a tour for it.
  • Closing times: Most big temples close around 17:00. Ginkaku-ji shortens its hours from 1 December. Kiyomizu-dera stays open late from 21 to 30 November 2026.
  • Manners: Do not photograph geiko and maiko without permission, and do not eat while walking in Nishiki Market.
  • Arriving? See our Kyoto Station guide if you come by train, or our Kansai Airport guide if you fly in.

Private guide and car for the day

A private driver-guide suits families, small groups and anyone short of time, because Kyoto's sights are far apart and the buses fill up. Listings differ a lot, so check four things before you book: the language of the guide, the list of stops, whether entrance fees are included, and where pick-up happens. On the listings we read on 19 November 2026, days run 9 to 10 hours, usually cover Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, Arashiyama and Gion, and some add Kinkaku-ji, Nishiki Market or Nara. Entrance fees at paid sights such as Kiyomizu-dera and Kinkaku-ji were marked as extra on at least one listing.

Private days in Kyoto with a guide and vehicle

Private days with a guide and vehicle. Pick-up rules differ, so read each one.

Pick-up: one listing we saw offers pick-up from hotels and apartments only, not from stations or airports, while another meets you at the Central Exit of Kyoto Station. Confirm the meeting point before you pay.

Tea ceremony and kimono

A tea ceremony is one of the easiest ways to slow down in Kyoto and sit inside a traditional house. The listings we read are short, usually 45 to 60 minutes. You watch the host prepare matcha, learn the basic etiquette, and whisk a bowl yourself, with a seasonal sweet. Several are in townhouses in the Higashiyama area near Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka, a short walk from Kiyomizu-dera, so they fit well before or after the temple.

Tea ceremony experiences in Kyoto

Short sessions in traditional townhouses, in English.

  • Kimono add-on: some tea ceremonies offer a kimono option, and the listing we read asks you to arrive 20 minutes early if you add it. Kimono and tea packages run 1.5 to 2 hours.
  • Dress code: the same listing says there is none for the plain tea ceremony.
  • Private or small group: several listings offer a private session, which suits families.

Day trips from Kyoto

Two day trips come up most often. Nara is short and easy by train. Amanohashidate and Ine Funaya on the Sea of Japan coast are much farther, and most visitors book a tour for them.

Nara: Todai-ji and the deer

Nara is about 35 to 45 minutes from Kyoto, depending on the train. JR Nara and Kintetsu-Nara are different stations, and Visit Nara gives a 22-minute walk from Kintetsu-Nara to Todai-ji. The Great Buddha Hall opens 8:00 to 17:00 from November to March, and 7:30 to 17:30 from April to October, with an entrance fee. Todai-ji's own site calls it the largest wooden building in the world. Visit Nara says about 1,400 wild deer live around Nara Park. They are protected as natural monuments, and the special deer crackers (shika senbei) sold in the park are the only food you may give them. If you have no food, show your empty hands and they usually leave you alone. Read the official Nara deer page before you go.

Amanohashidate and Ine Funaya

Amanohashidate is a pine-covered sandbar across Miyazu Bay in northern Kyoto Prefecture. JNTO calls it one of Japan's three scenic views, with Matsushima and Miyajima. The sandbar is 3.6 kilometres long, 20 metres wide at the narrowest point and 170 metres at the widest. From Amanohashidate Station it is about 6 minutes on foot to the sandbar and 12 minutes to the Amanohashidate Viewland lookout.

Ine is a small fishing town of about 1,900 people on Ine Bay, known for its funaya, boat houses built at the water's edge. JNTO says it can be reached only by car or by bus from Amanohashidate Station, about 60 minutes, roughly once an hour. The bus company Tango Kairiku Kotsu also lists a limited-time sightseeing express between Miyazu or Amanohashidate and Ine that needs no reservation, so check its timetable page for dates.

By public transport the train alone is 4 to 5 hours round trip to Amanohashidate, plus about 2 hours by bus to Ine and back, which is why many visitors choose a tour. The day tours we read run about 10 hours, with about 2 to 2.5 hours each way by van or bus. Some also stop at Miyama Kayabuki-no-Sato, a village of thatched-roof farmhouses, and many add the Ine Bay cruise or the cable car at Amanohashidate for an extra fee.

The main sights in the city

Opening times below are from each site's own pages on 19 November 2026. Temples change hours for events and seasons, so check the site for your date.

Fushimi Inari Taisha

Entry is free, and JNTO says you can visit at any time of day. The path up Mount Inari is lined with about 10,000 torii gates, and JNTO gives 2 to 3 hours for the full walk. It suggests going early, when fewer people are around, or after dark when the shrine is lit up. From JR Inari Station it is a 3-minute walk to the shrine.

Kiyomizu-dera

The temple opens at 6:00. In 2026 it closes at 18:00 for most of the year, and at 18:30 in July and August. The autumn special night viewing runs from 21 to 30 November 2026, with the temple open until 21:30 and last entry at 21:00, and no reservation is needed. The Jojuin garden is also open specially on those dates. An entrance fee applies. Read the temple's own schedule before you go.

Kinkaku-ji (the Golden Pavilion)

Kinkaku-ji, officially Rokuon-ji, is open 9:00 to 17:00 all year, although hours may change for special exhibitions. UNESCO listed it in 1994. A monk burned the pavilion in 1950 and it was rebuilt in 1955. The path is wheelchair accessible up to the pavilion, but the garden beyond has steps. See the temple's access page.

Ginkaku-ji (the Silver Pavilion)

Hours are 8:30 to 17:00 from 1 March to 30 November, and 9:00 to 16:30 from 1 December to the end of February. Normally only the gardens can be visited, not the buildings.

Nijo Castle

The castle grounds open at 8:45, with last entry at 16:00 and closing at 17:00. Ninomaru Palace admits visitors until 16:10. The castle is closed 29 to 31 December. Ninomaru Palace is also closed every Tuesday in December, from 26 to 28 December, and from 1 to 3 January. On Sunday 6 December 2026, a commemorative event marks 400 years since the Imperial visit of 1626, and last admission is brought forward to 12:00 for the grounds and 12:10 for Ninomaru Palace. See the castle's hours page.

Arashiyama

Arashiyama is the western district known for the bamboo grove, Tenryu-ji and the Togetsukyo Bridge. From Kyoto Station it is about 11 minutes by Rapid on the JR Sagano Line. Kyoto City's visitor guidelines include a request to protect the bamboo, and Kyoto's tourism site lists Arashiyama among the busy areas where you should not block the road.

Nishiki Market

Nishiki Market is a 400-metre street between Teramachi and Takakura, known as the Kitchen of Kyoto. Eating while walking through it is prohibited. Eat at the shop where you bought the food, or in the eating areas.

Manners in Gion and at temples

Kyoto's tourism site asks visitors to follow a few rules, mostly because people live in the busiest areas.

  • Geiko and maiko: do not stop, touch, follow or photograph them without permission. A maiko walking in the street usually has an appointment and is in a hurry.
  • Photography: it is prohibited in many Gion areas, including some private alleys, in parts of shrines and temples, and in some restaurants. Hanamikoji and Gion-Shirakawa are residential areas, so do not photograph residents.
  • Do not enter private property: the residents of southern Gion ask visitors to stay out of places other than shops and roads.
  • Do not block the road: Kiyomizudera Monzen, Nishiki Market and Arashiyama are busy, so step aside to take photos.
  • Eating and smoking: do not eat or smoke while walking on Hanamikoji. Smoking on the street is banned in Kyoto outside designated areas, and Kyoto Station, Kiyomizu and Gion are non-smoking areas.
  • Drones: most of Kyoto City is a densely populated area where drone flying is prohibited under Japan's Civil Aeronautics Act.

The rules are on Kyoto's responsible travel page.

What to eat

Kyoto was the imperial capital for more than 1,200 years, and its food shows it. JNTO and Kyoto's tourism site point to these first:

  • Kyo-kaiseki: a multi-course meal of seasonal ingredients, carefully prepared. Book ahead for dinner.
  • Yudofu and yuba: simmered tofu and tofu skin. Kyoto's tourism site lists several yudofu restaurants, including one at the Nanzen-ji gate.
  • Obanzai: everyday Kyoto home cooking with local vegetables. Many small restaurants change the dishes daily.
  • Shojin ryori: the vegetarian meal of Buddhist temples.
  • Nishiki Market: shops for rolled omelettes, hamo tempura, namafu and more. Remember the rule about eating at the shop.

In Kyoto it is polite to start an evening with a glass of sake, according to Kyoto's tourism site.

Getting to and around Kyoto

  • By bullet train: you arrive at Kyoto Station. Our Kyoto Station guide explains the Central and Hachijo sides, the platforms and the buses.
  • By plane: most international flights use Kansai Airport, and the JR Haruka reaches Kyoto Station. See our Kansai Airport guide.
  • Trains to the sights: Fushimi Inari is 5 minutes on the JR Nara Line. Arashiyama is about 11 minutes on the JR Sagano Line. Nara is 35 to 45 minutes.
  • City buses: they reach Kinkaku-ji, Ginkaku-ji and the Higashiyama area. Kyoto City says they accept cash and IC cards such as ICOCA, Suica and PiTaPa, as well as the 1-day pass.
  • Subway: the Karasuma Line runs north to south underground from Kyoto Station.
  • On foot: Higashiyama and Gion are best explored on foot. Allow more time for slopes and steps at Kiyomizu-dera and Fushimi Inari.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get from Kyoto to Nara?

Visit Nara gives about 41 minutes by JR Rapid from Kyoto Station to JR Nara Station, and about 34 minutes by Kintetsu Limited Express to Kintetsu-Nara. The Kintetsu train needs a limited express ticket. Make sure you take the Rapid, because JNTO says the local train takes about 25 minutes longer.

How long does it take to reach Amanohashidate and Ine from Kyoto?

JNTO gives about 2 to 2.5 hours by Hashidate Limited Express from Kyoto Station to Amanohashidate Station. A bus from Amanohashidate Station to Ine takes about 60 minutes and runs roughly once an hour. Day tours from Kyoto run about 10 hours in total.

What time does Fushimi Inari close?

Fushimi Inari Taisha can be visited at any time of day, and entry is free, according to JNTO and Kyoto's tourism site. The full walk up the mountain takes 2 to 3 hours.

Is Kiyomizu-dera open in the evening in November 2026?

Yes, during the autumn special night viewing from 21 to 30 November 2026. The temple stays open until 21:30, with last entry at 21:00, and no reservation is needed. For most of the rest of 2026 it closes at 18:00.

What are the opening hours of Kinkaku-ji, Ginkaku-ji and Nijo Castle?

Kinkaku-ji is open 9:00 to 17:00 all year. Ginkaku-ji is open 8:30 to 17:00 until 30 November, then 9:00 to 16:30 from 1 December. The grounds of Nijo Castle open at 8:45, with last entry at 16:00 and closing at 17:00, and the castle is closed 29 to 31 December. Last admission is at 12:00 on Sunday 6 December 2026.

Can I photograph geiko and maiko in Gion?

Not without permission. Kyoto's tourism site asks visitors not to stop, touch, follow or photograph geiko and maiko, and photography is prohibited in many Gion areas, including some private alleys.

What does a private guide and car in Kyoto include?

On the listings we read on 19 November 2026, the vehicle and driver-guide are included, days run 9 to 10 hours, and entrance fees at paid sights can be extra. Languages and pick-up points differ by listing, and one listing offers hotel pick-up only, not stations.
