Kyoto Station on one page

  • Two sides: the Central Exit (north, by Kyoto Tower) has the city buses, taxis and JR local lines. The Hachijo Exit (south) is the easiest for the Shinkansen (bullet trains) and the airport buses.
  • Shinkansen platforms: 11 to 14.
  • Local JR platforms: 4 to 7 for Osaka, 8 to 10 for Nara and Fushimi Inari, 31 to 33 for Arashiyama (far from the others), 0, 2 and 3 for Shiga. The Haruka to Kansai Airport uses 6, 7 or 30. Numbers can change: check the boards.
  • Subway: the Karasuma Line runs underground, north to south.
  • City buses: from in front of the Central Exit. The Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass is 1,100 yen for adults and works only on the day you first use it.
  • Tourist information: Kyo-Navi, 2nd floor, 8:30 am to 7 pm all year.
  • Luggage: coin lockers, and counters that store bags or send them to your hotel. Crosta KYOTO (B1) is open 8 am to 8 pm.
  • Need help? Ask station staff or Kyo-Navi. In an emergency, call 119.

Which side: Central or Hachijo?

Kyoto Station is one huge building with two main exits to the outside. The Central Exit is on the north side, by Kyoto Tower. The Hachijo Exit is on the south side. Kyoto City's tourism site compares them with the Marunouchi and Yaesu exits at Tokyo Station. JNTO's tip: the Shinkansen Hachijo entrance on the south is the most convenient for the Shinkansen, and the Central Gate on the north is the most convenient for JR lines and the Haruka Express to Kansai Airport.

One thing that trips people up: the Shinkansen concourse has its own exits, with names that sound like the main ones. The Shinkansen Central Exit is not the same as the Central Exit of the local-line station, according to Kyoto City, so follow the signs for the place you want rather than the word “Central”.

Where the trains take you

  • Fushimi Inari. JR West's Kyoto map gives 5 minutes on the Nara Line to Inari Station.
  • Arashiyama. About 11 minutes by Rapid service to Saga-Arashiyama (Kyoto City). JR West's map gives 9 to 10 minutes to Emmachi on the same line.
  • Osaka. About 30 minutes by JR Special Rapid Service between Kyoto and Osaka Station (Kyoto City).
  • Nara by JR. The JR Miyakoji Rapid takes about 45 minutes (Nara City Tourism Association). Make sure you board the Rapid: JNTO says the local train takes about 25 minutes longer.
  • Nara by Kintetsu. The Kintetsu limited express from Kintetsu Kyoto Station takes about 35 minutes (Nara City Tourism Association). Kintetsu says all limited express seats are reserved and you need a limited express ticket on top of the basic fare. JNTO says Kintetsu Nara Station is closer to the city centre than JR Nara Station, which is about a 20-minute walk from Nara Park.
  • Kansai Airport. The Haruka takes about 75 minutes (Kansai Airport's own page). See the leaving section and our Kansai Airport guide.
Finding the Kintetsu gate: from the Shinkansen concourse, leave through the Shinkansen Central Exit. From the local JR lines, leave through the West Exit and turn left (Kyoto City).

Passes and paying

  • Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass. 1,100 yen for adults and 550 yen for children. It covers all city subway lines and city buses, and most Kyoto Bus, Keihan Bus and West Japan JR Bus routes. It does not last 24 hours: it works only on the day you first use it. It is sold at Kyo-Navi, the Bus Information Office and vending machines (Kyoto City). A subway-only pass costs 800 yen.
  • IC cards. Kyoto City says city buses accept cash and IC cards such as ICOCA, Suica and PiTaPa, as well as the 1-day pass. Look for the card reader.
  • Getting on the bus. On regular city buses, board by the rear door and pay when you get off at the front. On the EX100 and EX101, board by the front door and pay as you board, then get off by the rear door.
  • Information. The Kyoto Station Bus Information Office (in front of the Central entrance) and the Kotochika Kyoto subway information centre (next to Central Gate 1) are open 7:30 am to 7:30 pm all year. They answer questions about buses and subways.
  • To Kiyomizu-dera. Kyoto City's tourism site names city buses 86 and 206 to the Gojozaka stop. It also suggests the subway to Kujo Station as a way to avoid the busiest roads.

Sources: Kyoto City Transportation Bureau pages for the 1-day pass, buses, the subway, the limited express bus and information centres, plus Kyoto City: 6 Tips. Checked 19 November 2026. Fares and passes change, so check the operator.

Seven Bank says its ATMs accept cards issued outside Japan with the Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, American Express, JCB, Discover and Diners Club marks, but some cards with these marks may not be accepted, and fees vary by card brand. Visa and Mastercard work round the clock; the other brands stop for a few minutes around midnight. With a card from abroad you can take out up to 100,000 yen at a time (30,000 yen for magnetic-stripe transactions).

The Kyoto Station Building also lists automatic exchange machines at JR Kyoto Isetan and Hotel Granvia Kyoto. These hours are from the station building’s FAQ. We could not check Travelex’s own site.
  • Post office. Kyoto Central Post Office: leave by the Central Exit (Kyoto Tower side) and turn left. JNTO says Japan Post Bank ATMs, most of them in post offices, take international cards. We did not check this one.

Lockers and luggage delivery

Kyoto's buses and trains are busy, and Kyoto City asks visitors to avoid taking large suitcases on them where they can. You have three choices: a coin locker, a storage counter, or sending the bag ahead to your hotel.

  • Coin lockers. JR West's station map marks coin lockers and a baggage room at Kyoto Station. Hands Free Kyoto says coin lockers can be used whenever the station is open, and some let you check vacancies and pay with an IC card. Kyoto City's Kyoto Station Smart Navi, a web tool for your phone, shows real-time locker vacancies. Lockers fill up on busy days.
  • Counters in the station. Hands Free Kyoto lists these, with the reception hours it prints (see the table below). It says that if you finish the paperwork during opening hours, you can drop off and pick up on the same day.
  • Station to station. JR West says Crosta's luggage delivery runs between Kyoto, Shin-Osaka, Osaka, Hiroshima and Hakata stations, and its Same-day Delivery WEST sends bags on the Shinkansen.
  • Temporary counters. In busy seasons Kyoto City sets up an extra luggage point. The spring 2026 one was in the station plaza in front of Hotel Granvia Kyoto, and a notice dated 29 October 2026 lists another temporary point at the station. Check the dates on the Hands Free Kyoto site.
  • Prices. The Hands Free Kyoto page does not list fees, so we give none. Ask at the counter before you hand over a bag.
  • Book online. There is an Osaka and Kyoto luggage delivery service, a partner link. Check the listing for pick-up points, sizes, prices and cut-off times.
  • Have these ready: your hotel name, address and phone number, written down.
  • Keep with you: medicines, valuables, chargers and a change of clothes.

Help for older travellers and anyone who needs assistance

You do not have to cope alone. Asking early is easier than struggling.

  • Tourist information. Kyo-Navi is on the 2nd floor of the Kyoto Station Building, in the north-south passage outside the ticket gates (8:30 am to 7 pm, all year), with staff who speak English, Chinese and Korean. The Bus Information Office in front of the Central entrance is open 7:30 am to 7:30 pm.
  • Lifts and toilets. JR West's station map marks lifts, escalators and wheelchair-accessible toilets. Kyoto City's tourism site says escalators, lifts and a multipurpose restroom are just outside the Shinkansen Central Transfer exit.
  • The subway with big bags. Kyoto City recommends the Underground East Exit route to the Karasuma Line for travellers with large luggage and older travellers.
  • Station staff. Tell staff at a ticket gate or ticket office if you need help or are not sure which way to go.
  • Send your suitcase ahead. See the luggage section above: you can have it delivered to your hotel.
  • Visitor hotline. The Japan Visitor Hotline, 050-3816-2787, is open 24 hours and helps in English, Chinese and Korean. In an emergency, call 119.

Leaving Kyoto by train or bus

  • Know your side. Bullet trains and the airport limousine bus are on the Hachijo side. Local JR trains, the Haruka and city buses are on the Central side.
  • To Kansai Airport. The Haruka limited express takes about 75 minutes (Kansai Airport's own page) and uses platform 6, 7 or 30 (Kyoto City). The airport limousine bus leaves from stop H2 at the Hachijo Exit, takes about 1 hour 25 to 1 hour 30 minutes to Terminal 1 and costs 2,800 yen one way (bus company's fare). We have left out the discounted WEST QR HARUKA fare, because the JR West pages we read disagree on the current price. See our Kansai Airport guide.
  • Haruka tickets online. You can book a JR Haruka ticket (partner link) with Kyoto Station departures. It is for visitors with Temporary Visitor status, not for Japanese passport holders or permanent residents.

  • To Nara. The JR Miyakoji Rapid takes about 45 minutes, and the Kintetsu limited express about 35 minutes.
  • To Tokyo. The fastest Shinkansen take about 130 minutes (JR Central).
  • Allow time for the station itself. Kyoto Station is big, and the Sagano Line platforms for Arashiyama are far from the others. We did not find official walking times between gates, so we give none: add a buffer, especially with luggage.
  • Last trains. We did not confirm last-train times, so check the timetable for your route before a late evening journey.
  • Taking a Shinkansen? Check your platform on the departure board, and your train name against your pass. The Japan Rail Pass is not valid on Nozomi and Mizuho.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which exit should I use at Kyoto Station: Central or Hachijo?

Kyoto Station has two big exits to the outside: the Central Exit on the north side, by Kyoto Tower, and the Hachijo Exit on the south side. Kyoto City's tourism site compares them with the Marunouchi and Yaesu exits at Tokyo Station. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) says the Shinkansen Hachijo entrance on the south side is the most convenient for the Shinkansen, while the Central Gate on the north side is the most convenient for JR lines and the Haruka Express to Kansai Airport.

I arrived by Shinkansen. How do I get to a city bus or the subway?

City buses leave from in front of the Central Exit, at the foot of Kyoto Tower. For the Karasuma Line subway, Kyoto City's tourism site lists three routes from the Shinkansen concourse: out through the Shinkansen Hachijo Exit or the Hachijo East Exit, then left and underground, or out through the Underground East Exit on the north side. It recommends the Underground East Exit route for travellers with large luggage or older travellers. That page's route details are dated October 2022, so follow the signs in the station as well.

Which platforms are the Shinkansen at Kyoto Station?

JR Central's train information lists platforms 11 to 14 for Kyoto on the Tokaido Shinkansen. Your ticket and the departure boards show your platform, and platforms can change. JR Central says the fastest Shinkansen take about 130 minutes from Tokyo to Kyoto. Kyoto City's tourism site gives about 34 minutes from Nagoya on the Nozomi and about 15 minutes from Shin-Osaka.

Which platform is the Haruka to Kansai Airport?

Kyoto City's tourism site lists platforms 6, 7 and 30 for the Haruka limited express to Kansai Airport, and warns that platform numbers may change, so check the departure board. Kansai Airport's own page gives about 75 minutes from Kansai Airport to Kyoto. JNTO says the Central Gate on the north side is the most convenient for the Haruka.

Which platforms are the local JR trains?

Kyoto City's tourism site lists these (numbers may change): platforms 0, 2 and 3 for the Biwako and Kosei lines toward Yamashina and Shiga; 4 to 7 for the Kyoto Line toward Takatsuki and Osaka; 8 to 10 for the Nara Line toward Inari and Uji; and 31 to 33 for the Sagano (San-in) Line toward Saga-Arashiyama. It adds that the Sagano platforms are a long way from the other lines.

How do I get from Kyoto Station to Nara?

There are two ways. The JR Miyakoji Rapid on the JR Nara Line takes about 45 minutes, according to the Nara City Tourism Association (another Nara tourism page says 41). The Kintetsu limited express from Kintetsu Kyoto Station takes about 35 minutes, and Kintetsu says you need a limited express ticket on top of the basic fare, with all seats reserved. JNTO says Kintetsu Nara Station is closer to the city centre than JR Nara Station, which is about a 20-minute walk from Nara Park.

How do I get to Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama?

JR West's Kyoto map gives 5 minutes from Kyoto to Inari Station on the JR Nara Line for Fushimi Inari. For Arashiyama, take the JR Sagano Line: Kyoto City's tourism site says Saga-Arashiyama takes about 11 minutes by Rapid service, and JR West's map gives 9 to 10 minutes to Emmachi on the same line.

How do I use the subway and city buses?

The Karasuma Line runs north to south from Kyoto Station, and Karasuma Oike is where it meets the Tozai Line. JR West's map gives 6 minutes to Karasuma Oike and 14 to Kitaoji. Subway fares are 220 to 360 yen by distance, and city buses in the flat-fare zone cost 230 yen for adults (Kyoto City Transportation Bureau). Subway and city bus: the Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass is 1,100 yen for adults and 550 yen for children, and it works only on the day you first use it, not for 24 hours.

Which bus goes to Kiyomizu-dera, Gion and Ginkaku-ji?

The Sightseeing Limited Express Bus EX100 and EX101 leave from Kyoto Station and serve Gojozaka (for Kiyomizu-dera), Gion, the Okazaki Park and Heian Jingu stop and Ginkaku-ji, for 500 yen a ride, or free with the 1-day pass (Kyoto City Transportation Bureau). Kyoto City's tourism site names the regular city buses 86 and 206 to the Gojozaka stop. On the EX buses you board at the front door and pay as you board; on other city buses you board at the rear door and pay when you get off.

Where can I leave my luggage?

Kyoto Station has coin lockers, and Hands Free Kyoto (run by Kyoto City) lists luggage counters that store bags or send them to your hotel, including Crosta KYOTO on B1 (8 am to 8 pm) and the JR Kyoto Station Hachijo Gate Delivery Service on 1F (10 am to 6 pm). Kyoto City also sets up temporary counters in busy seasons: a notice dated 29 October 2026 lists a temporary point at the station. Check the Hands Free Kyoto site for dates and fees.

Where is the tourist information, and when is it open?

Kyoto Tourist Information Center Kyo-Navi is on the 2nd floor of the Kyoto Station Building, in the north-south passage outside the ticket gates. It is open 8:30 am to 7 pm all year, with staff who speak English, Chinese and Korean. The Kyoto Station Bus Information Office in front of the Central entrance and the Kotochika Kyoto subway information centre next to Central Gate 1 are both open 7:30 am to 7:30 pm all year.

How do I get from Kyoto Station to Kansai Airport?

The Haruka limited express takes about 75 minutes. The airport limousine bus (KATE) leaves from stop H2 at the Hachijo Exit, takes about 1 hour 25 to 1 hour 30 minutes to Terminal 1, and costs 2,800 yen one way according to the bus company. Our Kansai Airport guide has the details. Fares and times change, so check the operator.

Does the Japan Rail Pass work on every Shinkansen from Kyoto?

No. Kyoto City's tourism site says the Japan Rail Pass cannot be used on the Nozomi Shinkansen. Check the train name on your ticket and your pass before you board.
