📋 Visa & Documents for Indians

Indian citizens can currently enter Malaysia without a visa for tourism, short business or family visits of up to 30 days. This exemption is in force until 31 December 2026, so check the Malaysian Immigration Department or the High Commission before booking trips beyond that date. The old eNTRI scheme is not needed for visa-free stays, but you must still file the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online.

💡 Pro Tip: File the MDAC only on the official Immigration Department site (imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main). It is free, so never pay a third-party site. It can only be submitted within 3 days before you arrive, so do it 1–2 days before your flight. At immigration, officers can ask for a return or onward ticket, a hotel booking and proof of funds, so carry them (printed or on your phone).
⚠️ Passport Check: Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from your travel date. If you're planning a short trip and your passport is expiring soon, renew it before applying for the visa. Malaysia immigration is strict about this requirement.

Malaysia has a tropical climate with high humidity year-round. However, weather differs significantly between the west coast (Kuala Lumpur, Penang) and east coast (Terengganu, Pahang). Monsoon seasons are crucial to know.

👚 Complete Malaysia Packing List

Clothing (Modest & Heat-Friendly)

  • 5–6 breathable shirts/tops — Cotton or moisture-wicking. Lightweight is critical
  • 2–3 shorts and light pants — Shorts are fine everywhere except mosques and temples (wear pants or long skirts)
  • 1–2 long dresses/tunics — For visiting Batu Caves temple and blue mosques. Sleeves required (cover shoulders)
  • 1 light jacket or hoodie — Mall and restaurant AC can be intense. Also useful for evening cool-downs
  • 1 swimsuit — Essential for Langkawi beaches, Perhentian Islands, and hotel pools
  • Comfortable walking shoes — For KL's bustling streets. Waterproof preferred
  • Flip-flops or sandals — For beaches and quick errands. Many Malaysians wear slides
  • 1 hijab or scarf — Carry one even if you don't usually wear one. Needed for mosque visits (borrowed scarves available at entrances)
💡 Temple/Mosque Dress Code: When entering mosques or Hindu temples (like Batu Caves), wear full-length pants or skirts and cover your shoulders. Shoes are removed at entrances. Many temples provide wrap-around cloths for ₹10–50 if you forget. Better to carry a lightweight sarong or scarf in your day bag.

Toiletries & Health

  • Sunscreen SPF 50+ — Malaysia's sun is stronger than India's. Reapply every 2 hours at beaches
  • Mosquito repellent — Dengue, Zika, and malaria exist in Malaysia. Use repellent (DEET-based) especially at dusk
  • After-sun care lotion — Aloe vera gel for inevitable sunburn
  • Basic medicines — Paracetamol, antacids, diarrhea medicine (Imodium). Watson's pharmacies are everywhere
  • Deodorant — Non-negotiable in 80% humidity
  • Hair ties and hair oil — Humidity will be your hair's enemy

Electronics & Money

  • Power adapter — Type G plugs (UK-style, 3 rectangular pins). Same as Singapore/Dubai. Indian plugs don't fit
  • Power bank — Essential for full-day city tours and island trips
  • Forex card or dual-currency card — Load MYR for better exchange rates. Cards like HDFC EasyShop or yes Global International work everywhere
  • Cash (Malaysian Ringgit) — Some street food vendors and taxis prefer cash. Exchange at banks, not airport counters

⚠️ Malaysia Customs & Laws to Know

Malaysia is generally traveler-friendly but has strict rules worth respecting:

  • Alcohol limits — You can bring 1 litre of spirits/wine or 12 cans of beer into Malaysia. Exceeding these limits incurs heavy fines
  • No drugs (zero tolerance) — Even small quantities lead to severe penalties, including death penalty for trafficking. Absolutely non-negotiable
  • No disrespecting Islam — Malicious remarks about Islam, the Sultan, or Islam's role in Malaysia can result in imprisonment. Be respectful
  • No public displays of affection — While not enforced heavily, kissing or prolonged hugging in public is frowned upon in conservative areas
  • Dress respectfully in religious areas — Covered shoulders and knees required for temples and mosques
⚠️ Drug Laws: Malaysia's drug laws are among the world's strictest. Even carrying recreational drugs (CBD, vapes with THC, herbal products containing controlled substances) can result in arrest, prosecution, and imprisonment for years. Leave everything at home.

🎯 Quick Travel Tips

  • Halal food is everywhere — Even non-Muslim restaurants are halal-friendly. Look for the Halal certification mark
  • Petaling Jaya and Bukit Bintang for Indian food — Taste of India restaurant chain and countless small eateries
  • Batu Caves dress code — Wear covered clothes (long pants/skirt, covered shoulders). Climbing the 272 steps takes 10 minutes. Free entry
  • Penang Georgetown is a UNESCO site — Amazing street art, colonial buildings, and street food capital. Worth 2 days
  • Langkawi is duty-free — Alcohol and electronics are cheaper. Many Indians buy duty-free stuff here
  • Night markets (pasar malam) — Food, clothes, souvenirs. Bargaining is expected and accepted unlike malls
  • Train travel — KL to Penang or KL to Kuala Terengganu by train is scenic and affordable (MYR 50–100 or ₹950–1,900). Book train tickets (partner link)

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Malaysia?
No, not for stays of up to 30 days. Indian passport holders can enter Malaysia visa-free for tourism for up to 30 days, and the exemption is currently valid until 31 December 2026 (check whether it has been extended if you travel later). You must file the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days before arrival, and carry a passport valid for at least 6 months, a return or onward ticket, accommodation details and proof of sufficient funds. Longer stays, work or study need a visa.
What should I pack for Malaysia from India?
Pack lightweight, breathable clothes, modest clothing for mosque visits (Putrajaya, KL), comfortable walking shoes, an umbrella (afternoon showers are common), mosquito repellent, sunscreen, swimwear for Langkawi and island trips, and a light jacket for air-conditioned malls and the Cameron Highlands (which can be cool).
What is the best time to visit Malaysia from India?
Malaysia can be visited year-round. For peninsular Malaysia (KL, Penang, Langkawi), December to April is driest. For Malaysian Borneo (Sabah, Sarawak), March to October is best. Avoid the east coast (Perhentian Islands) during November-February monsoon. June-August is peak season. Direct flights from India take 4-5 hours.
How much does a Malaysia trip cost from India?
A 7-day Malaysia trip costs approximately ₹30,000-50,000 per person on a budget, ₹50,000-1,00,000 mid-range, and ₹1,00,000+ luxury. Return flights from India cost ₹12,000-25,000 (AirAsia offers some of the cheapest fares). Street food in KL costs ₹100-250 per meal. Malaysia is very affordable, especially compared to Singapore.
